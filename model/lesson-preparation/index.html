Lesson preparation

Plan from the learning goal, anticipate barriers and decide how pupils will think.

Lessons are deliberately planned to align with curriculum intent and to sequence knowledge effectively. Teachers maintain high expectations for all learners, with particular attention to disadvantaged pupils and those with SEND. The overarching goal is to develop student expertise and independence over time. Teachers use their knowledge of students to anticipate barriers - such as gaps in prior knowledge, misconceptions, or individual needs - and plan proactively to address them, reducing the need for reactive adaptation during lessons. This includes anticipating barriers linked to speech and language, sensory processing, motor development, executive functioning, and social/emotional development (see appendix 4). Planning is informed by the BSCS model and its underpinning strategies, ensuring: high levels of participation and thinking clear, explicit instruction (including precise explanations, modelling, and small, sequenced steps) responsiveness to student understanding through frequent checking and adaptive teaching Resources are designed to be accessible to all learners Other adults in the room are effectively used (e.g. teaching assistants)

Techniques in the BSCS model

Backwards planning/ Begin with the End; Plan the means of participation Plan the exemplar; Plan the error; Seating plans; Planning for the use of teaching assistants

Professional judgement

Select and sequence techniques in response to the curriculum content and the pupils. The model may repeat within one lesson or span several lessons. These techniques are not a checklist to use all at once.
