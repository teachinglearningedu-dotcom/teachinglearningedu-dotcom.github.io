Feedback principles at BSCS

At BSCS, effective feedback is underpinned by the following principles:

Feedback begins with high-quality instruction. Clear explanations, modelling, guided practice and checks for understanding reduce the amount of feedback needed later. Feedback should not compensate for unclear teaching; it should build on strong lesson preparation, explicit instruction and responsive teaching.

Feedback moves learning forward. Feedback should target specific learning gaps, misconceptions or next steps. The most effective feedback focuses on the task, the subject knowledge or the student’s self-regulation strategies, rather than vague praise or personal characteristics.

Feedback is timely and proportionate. Feedback may be immediate, delayed, verbal, written, whole-class, peer-led or supported by technology. Teachers use professional judgement to choose the most appropriate method based on the task, the class and the learning need. Policies should not over-specify the frequency or method of feedback.

Students must act on feedback. Feedback is only effective when students understand it and have time to use it. Teachers should plan opportunities for students to correct, redraft, rehearse, practise or extend their work so that the feedback loop is closed.

Feedback should be manageable and sustainable. Written marking is one possible method, but it is not inherently better than verbal feedback, whole-class feedback, live feedback or structured peer assessment. Feedback approaches should be effective for students and proportionate for staff workload.

In practice, feedback at BSCS may include live verbal feedback during practice, whole-class feedback following common misconceptions, modelling and re-modelling under the visualiser, show call, structured peer assessment, self-correction, redrafting, targeted written comments, or adaptive re-teaching. The chosen approach should always align with the BSCS lesson phase and the intended learning outcome: check understanding, adapt teaching, secure learning through practice, and ensure students improve their work
