Climate & Culture

Build warm relationships and consistent routines that protect attention.

Relationships: Positive culture with high expectations and high success. Teacher–student relationships are characterised by mutual respect, trust, warmth, and consistency. Students feel safe to think hard, participate, and make mistakes. Teachers actively build motivation through a sense of belonging, competence, and psychological safety, normalising error as part of learning. Securing attention: Attention is the gateway to learning. Teachers deliberately gain, maintain, and direct attention to the most important content. Distractions are minimised, and all students are fully focused before instruction begins, ensuring optimal conditions for learning. Routines: Consistent, well-established routines that maximise learning time, reduce cognitive load, and create a calm, purposeful environment. Routines are explicitly taught, practised, and reinforced to ensure automaticity and high expectations. Whole-school alignment of entry, exit, and attention routines provides consistency, enabling teachers to focus their cognitive effort on driving thinking in the classroom.

Techniques in the BSCS model

Threshold; Do Now; Engineering success; Signal for attention; Economy of language; 100%; Strong voice; What to do; The roll out; Check behavioural understanding; Radar/Be Seen Looking; Narrate the Positive; Correct where necessary; Prime; Timer; Directed Choice

Professional judgement

Select and sequence techniques in response to the curriculum content and the pupils. The model may repeat within one lesson or span several lessons. These techniques are not a checklist to use all at once.
