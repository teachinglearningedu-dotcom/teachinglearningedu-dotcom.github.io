Writing a precise goal

Turn a broad aim into one observable change: I will … so that students …

Your classroom-based Professional Growth focus should connect what you will do with what will be different for pupils. Use this structure:

I will [specific change in my practice] so that students [observable response or outcome].

Four steps to a precise goal

StepAsk yourselfExample
1. Pupil problemWhat are pupils doing that limits learning, or not yet doing that would improve it?Pupils in my lower-attaining classes are not engaging in Turn and Talk.
2. Possible causeWhy might this be happening? Consider knowledge, confidence, routines, modelling, scaffolding, practice or feedback. You do not need to be right yet.They may lack confidence, a clear routine or the knowledge to discuss.
3. Practice changeWhat one change could you make? Browse the pupil needs pages for options.Model a strong response, give a sentence stem and cold call pairs afterwards.
4. Write the goalDoes it link a teacher action to a pupil outcome?I will structure Turn and Talk with a modelled response and a sentence stem so that students give more developed answers.

From broad to precise

Too broadPrecise and observable
Improve my questioning.I will use question–pause–name cold calling with five seconds of thinking time so that more students prepare an answer and give fuller responses.
Use more checking for understanding.I will use a mini-whiteboard check after each explanation chunk so that I can see every student's answer and fix errors before independent practice.
Improve independent practice.I will protect at least 15 minutes for independent practice so that students complete enough practice and I can see what they can do without support.
Better scaffolding.I will fade prompts across worked examples so that students can reproduce the process independently and explain each step.

Avoid

  • Strategy-only goals such as "improve behaviour management", which say nothing about pupils.
  • Lists of several vague aims. Choose one focus you can observe.
  • Goals that cannot be seen in a lesson. A peer should be able to observe your change and pupils' responses.

Every strategy page includes an example goal in this format. Use it as a starting point and adapt it to your class.
