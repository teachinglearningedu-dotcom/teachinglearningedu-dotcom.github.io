Evaluating impact and deciding what next

Separate how well you implemented the change from its effect on pupils, then refine, embed or rethink.

The aim is not to prove that a strategy works, but to find out whether it is helping your pupils and what to do next. Keep two questions separate.

1. Am I doing it as intended?

This is implementation evidence: how consistently and precisely you are using the strategy. Peer observation and self-review help here.

2. Is it helping pupils?

This is impact evidence: what pupils now do, know or produce differently. Look at pupil responses and work.

Simple sources of evidence

  • Mini-whiteboard or hinge question responses on first attempt and re-check.
  • Do Now or quiz scores on revisited items over several weeks.
  • Samples of pupil work before and after the change.
  • Who contributes, and how fully, over several lessons.
  • Time taken for routines or for all pupils to start work.

Each strategy page suggests a quick check of impact. Choose one and use it consistently.

Interpret carefully

  • Busy, compliant pupils or completed tasks are not the same as secure learning.
  • Early changes may be small. Look for direction of travel across several lessons.
  • Other factors affect pupil outcomes. Avoid claiming a single change caused improvement in attainment.

Decide what next

If…Then…
The strategy is used well and pupils are respondingEmbed it, then extend it to another class or a related step.
The strategy is used inconsistentlyRefine the implementation. Practise the specific step, script it or use a prompt.
It is used well but pupils are not respondingRethink. Revisit the cause of the problem and choose a different strategy.
