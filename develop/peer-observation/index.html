Developmental peer observation

A short, focused protocol for observing one change and how pupils respond.

Peer observation in Individual Development is developmental, not judgemental. The observer gathers focused evidence on one change and how pupils respond. There are no grades and no whole-lesson judgements.

Before the observation (5 minutes)

  • Share your goal: I will … so that students …
  • Agree when in the lesson the strategy will be used.
  • Agree two or three look-fors. Each strategy page suggests some, covering what you will do and how pupils should respond.

During the observation

  • Focus only on the agreed part of the lesson and the agreed look-fors.
  • Record what you see and hear, not opinions: what the teacher said or did, and what pupils did in response.
  • Note examples of pupil responses: answers on boards, quality of talk, errors, how many pupils were involved.

After the observation (10 minutes)

  1. What did you notice? The observer shares evidence against each look-for.
  2. How did pupils respond? Discuss what the evidence suggests about pupils' learning.
  3. What next? Agree one precise refinement to try before the next observation.

Useful dialogue prompts

  • "When you did …, I saw pupils …"
  • "How many pupils were able to …?"
  • "What would you change about the timing or wording?"
  • "What will you look for in pupils' work this week?"

Keep observation notes developmental and between colleagues. Do not record identifiable pupil information beyond what is needed for professional discussion.
