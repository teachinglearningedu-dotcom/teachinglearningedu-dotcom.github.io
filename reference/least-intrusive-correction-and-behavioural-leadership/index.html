Least-intrusive correction and behavioural leadership

A calm graduated response that starts with visibility, proximity or non-verbal cues and escalates only as needed while preserving dignity and learning time.

What it is: A calm graduated response that starts with visibility, proximity or non-verbal cues and escalates only as needed while preserving dignity and learning time.

Why it helps: Low-intrusion responses prevent secondary conflict and keep attention on learning. Clear, predictable consequences and take-up time reduce debate and emotional escalation.

Use it when: low-level off-task behaviour, talking, lateness, refusal or argument that must be addressed without derailing instruction.

Implementation sequence

Position for visibility and use deliberate scanning.

Try proximity, eye contact, a gesture, self-interrupt or positive narration nearby.

If needed, describe the behaviour and direct the required action briefly.

Use private correction, partial agreement or a directed choice where appropriate.

Allow take-up time, then check compliance and apply the agreed sanction consistently.

Follow up and rebuild the relationship after the lesson.

DODON'T
Separate the primary behaviour from secondary reactions. Use a calm, firm tone and economy of language. Follow the school behaviour system consistently. Enter a public debate about fairness during instruction. Stack several corrections without allowing take-up time. Ignore serious or unsafe behaviour in the name of low intrusion.

Check for impact

Corrections become shorter and students return to learning quickly.

Repeated incidents reduce because follow-up and routines are consistent.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Behavioural Leadership Map, Behaviour Management Ten Tools and TCMH; StepFinder: Respond to Negative Behaviours.
