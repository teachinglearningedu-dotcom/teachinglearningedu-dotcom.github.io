Core idea

A learning episode is the time needed for pupils to grasp a novel idea. It does not have to fit neatly into one lesson: an episode may be shorter than a lesson or may span several lessons. Planning therefore starts with the learning, not the slide deck or activity sequence.

Prepare the learning, not the PowerPoint.

A practical planning sequence

  • 1. Define the key idea pupils should understand, not just the task they will complete.
  • 2. Identify the prerequisite knowledge needed to access that idea. This is where atomisation becomes especially useful.
  • 3. Plan the Core Questions or hinge points that will reveal whether pupils are ready to move on.
  • 4. Plan the instructional episode: Atomisation → I Do → We Do → Consolidation.
  • 5. Only then move to purposeful independent practice or problem-solving.
  • 6. Plan how the idea will be revisited later through future Do Nows, weekly mixed-topic low-stakes quizzes, mixed-topic homework, or deliberate interweaving with later content.

From initial security to transfer

The project summary captured a useful progression for planning practice beyond first success:

  • Secure: pupils can reproduce the new knowledge or procedure accurately.
  • Vary: change surface features while preserving the underlying idea.
  • Connect: link the idea to related knowledge and discriminate between similar cases.
  • Apply: use the learning in purposeful questions or contexts.
  • Transfer: use the idea in less familiar settings where the route is not immediately signposted.

Memory and retention

The learning episode is not complete simply because pupils were successful at the end of the lesson. The project summary explicitly linked planning to delayed retrieval: future Do Nows, mixed-topic low-stakes quizzes, mixed homework and interweaving previously taught content. This makes retention a planned feature of curriculum delivery rather than an afterthought.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Lesson preparation: This is the strongest fit: define the endpoint, prerequisite knowledge, sequence, checks and future retrieval before the lesson.
  • Explain: The episode deliberately plans where I Do / explicit explanation is needed and how much new material is introduced at once.
  • Check and adapt: Core Questions and hinge points provide the evidence for deciding whether to move on, re-explain or extend.
  • Secure learning through practice: Practice follows sufficient modelling and guided practice, and progresses from security towards transfer.
  • Do Now: Future Do Nows are explicitly planned as delayed retrieval opportunities.
