Mini-whiteboards for diagnosis

Using standardised, simultaneous board responses to gather valid whole-class data quickly enough to adapt in the moment.

What it is: Using standardised, simultaneous board responses to gather valid whole-class data quickly enough to adapt in the moment.

Why it helps: The teacher sees every response rather than extrapolating from a few voices. Controlled reveal and systematic scanning preserve independence and improve the validity of the sample.

Use it when: high-value short responses, calculations, diagrams, misconceptions, vocabulary and harder post-teach checks.

Implementation sequence

Plan the question, correct response and likely error patterns before teaching.

State the required format and give independent thinking/writing time.

Cue a simultaneous reveal; stagger only in a controlled way that prevents copying.

Scan systematically, including indicator students and the full field.

Give precise feedback and require correction.

Choose a responsive action and test it with a fresh board question.

DODON'T
Use a question-mark convention for genuine uncertainty. Keep boards visible long enough to scan but not to invite answer changing. Ask students to lower boards before explanation so attention returns. Ask questions too broad to interpret at a glance. Focus only on the first few boards or the most confident students. Reveal the correct answer before students commit.

Check for impact

Response data is independent, simultaneous and interpretable.

Rechecks show whether the chosen adaptation worked.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Mini-Whiteboards; Tips for Teachers: Mini Whiteboards and Responsive Teaching.
