Thinking aloud

Verbalising the expert's internal decisions, monitoring and self-correction while completing a task.

What it is: Verbalising the expert's internal decisions, monitoring and self-correction while completing a task.

Why it helps: Expert thinking is often invisible and automated. Narration reveals how to plan, select a strategy, notice difficulty, check progress and evaluate the result.

Use it when: tasks involving judgement, problem solving, reading comprehension, writing choices or strategic use of procedures.

Implementation sequence

Clarify the task and identify the decision points students cannot see.

Model a concise internal dialogue using first-person language.

Name the cue, relevant knowledge, option considered and reason for the choice.

Include a deliberate check or correction rather than presenting effortless expertise.

Invite students to explain the next decision, then practise their own think-aloud.

Fade the external narration into prompts for independent self-questioning.

DODON'T
Script key decision sentences in advance. Separate narration of thinking from description of physical actions. Use subject-specific language accurately. Narrate every obvious action and overload the model. Present expertise as instant intuition with no monitoring. Let students watch passively without a subsequent attempt.

Check for impact

Students can articulate why they selected a step or strategy.

Independent work shows more self-checking and fewer avoidable process errors.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Modelling Through Thinking Aloud; StepFinder: Think Aloud and Model Metacognitive Strategies.
