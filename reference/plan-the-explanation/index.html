Plan the explanation

Deliberately scripting the conceptual route, key language, examples, representations, misconceptions and stopping points before teaching new content.

What it is: Deliberately scripting the conceptual route, key language, examples, representations, misconceptions and stopping points before teaching new content.

Why it helps: Planning reduces avoidable ambiguity and frees the teacher to monitor attention and understanding during delivery. It also helps separate essential detail from interesting but distracting information.

Use it when: new or difficult content, explanations involving diagrams or demonstrations, and points where previous classes have developed predictable misconceptions.

Implementation sequence

Name the one idea students must understand by the end of the explanation.

Activate the minimum relevant prior knowledge and define unfamiliar vocabulary.

Chunk the explanation into coherent steps with one purpose per step.

Select examples, near-miss non-examples and representations that reveal the concept.

Script precise phrases, likely questions and checks for listening and understanding.

Rehearse aloud and remove any detail that does not serve the goal.

DODON'T
Plan where to pause, point, reveal and check. Use consistent terminology and sentence structures. Prepare an alternative representation for the likely sticking point. Rely on slides to do the explaining. Introduce several new ideas before checking the first. Add extra facts that increase load without increasing understanding.

Check for impact

Students can restate the key idea using the intended vocabulary.

Checks reveal understanding of the concept, not merely recall of the teacher's words.

Sources informing this profile: BSCS Policy Appendix 1; StepFinder; DistilED: Explaining with Precision and Science of Simplicity.
