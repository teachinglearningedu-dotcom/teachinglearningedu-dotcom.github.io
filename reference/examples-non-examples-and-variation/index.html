Examples, non-examples and variation

Choosing positive examples, near-miss non-examples and deliberately varied cases to reveal the defining features and boundaries of a concept.

What it is: Choosing positive examples, near-miss non-examples and deliberately varied cases to reveal the defining features and boundaries of a concept.

Why it helps: Examples alone can lead to over- or under-generalisation. Carefully controlled contrast focuses attention on the critical feature and supports transfer beyond a memorised surface form.

Use it when: concept formation, vocabulary, classification, quality criteria and misconceptions caused by superficial similarities.

Implementation sequence

Name the critical feature students must notice.

Choose a clear example and a near-miss non-example differing mainly on that feature.

Compare them side by side and ask what changed.

Use minimally different examples to isolate the feature, then maximally different examples to broaden the category.

Ask students to classify and justify new cases.

DODON'T
Keep irrelevant features constant during the first contrast. Ask for the reason a case belongs or does not belong. Include common misconceptions as purposeful non-examples. Use absurd non-examples with too many differences. Explain only what is wrong without clarifying the boundary. Show a single prototypical example and assume transfer.

Check for impact

Students classify unfamiliar cases using the defining feature.

Overgeneralisation and undergeneralisation decrease.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Non-Examples; StepFinder: Explain for Deeper Understanding.
