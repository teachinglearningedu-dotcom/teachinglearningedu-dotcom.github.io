Precise individual feedback

Brief information that identifies the exact gap and prompts the student to retrieve, correct, practise or improve while the work is still active.

What it is: Brief information that identifies the exact gap and prompts the student to retrieve, correct, practise or improve while the work is still active.

Why it helps: Feedback is useful only when it changes subsequent thinking or performance. Prompting before telling preserves student cognition and helps diagnose whether the issue is knowledge or execution.

Use it when: during practice when an error is local to an individual or small group and can be corrected without interrupting the class.

Implementation sequence

Compare the work with the planned success criteria or model.

Locate the error and decide whether the student can self-correct.

Use a prompt, question or raised-error-awareness cue first.

Give a clue, model a small step or direct action only as necessary.

Require the student to correct and complete additional matched practice.

Return to verify the improvement.

DODON'T
Praise the specific successful feature before addressing the gap where appropriate. Use precise verbs and point to the location of change. Turn recurring corrections into retrieval prompts. Give vague comments such as 'more detail' without an action. Complete the thinking or correction for the student. Assume verbal acknowledgement means the work improved.

Check for impact

The student makes an accurate correction and succeeds on a similar item.

The same error becomes less frequent in later independent work.
