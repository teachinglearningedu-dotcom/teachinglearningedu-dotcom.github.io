Backwards planning / Begin with the end

Planning from the intended curriculum outcome or performance and working backwards to the prerequisite knowledge, intermediate steps, practice and assessment evidence.

What it is: Planning from the intended curriculum outcome or performance and working backwards to the prerequisite knowledge, intermediate steps, practice and assessment evidence.

Why it helps: A clear endpoint improves alignment: explanations, tasks and checks are selected because they build the required knowledge rather than because they are convenient or engaging in isolation.

Use it when: planning a unit, a lesson sequence or any high-leverage task whose success depends on several knowledge steps.

Implementation sequence

Specify the precise end performance and the knowledge students must retrieve or apply.

Break the endpoint into prerequisite and component steps; identify the likely bottleneck.

Sequence examples and practice from secure foundations towards greater complexity.

Plan what evidence will show readiness to move between steps.

Review whether every activity and resource serves the endpoint.

DODON'T
Write an exemplar or success description before planning the route. Keep the endpoint ambitious and common while planning routes into it. Build retrieval and cumulative practice into the sequence. Plan a string of activities before defining what students will learn. Confuse task completion with mastery of the intended knowledge. Treat the plan as fixed when checks show a prerequisite gap.

Check for impact

Students can explain how each task connects to the intended outcome.

Later practice draws on knowledge deliberately secured earlier.

Sources informing this profile: BSCS Teaching and Learning Policy; StepFinder: Lesson Preparation and Optimise Communication.
