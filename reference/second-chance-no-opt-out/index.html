Second Chance / No Opt Out

Returning to a student who could not initially answer after giving them time and access to peer responses, so that they still complete an intellectually meaningful response.

What it is: Returning to a student who could not initially answer after giving them time and access to peer responses, so that they still complete an intellectually meaningful response.

Why it helps: A predictable return maintains accountability without humiliation and turns 'I don't know' into attentive listening, rehearsal and a successful second attempt.

Use it when: cold call, checks for listening and any questioning routine where students may genuinely be unsure or temporarily unable to formulate.

Implementation sequence

Acknowledge uncertainty neutrally and state that you will return.

Ask one or two peers for answers while keeping the original student listening.

Return and require the student to repeat, paraphrase, select, evaluate or add to an answer.

Praise persistence and the improved response, not merely compliance.

Follow up privately if repeated non-preparation reflects a routine or access problem.

DODON'T
Keep the tone warm and the return predictable. Choose a second-chance task the student can complete meaningfully. Use peer answers as models, not as a public rescue. Forget to return after saying you will. Turn the exchange into a prolonged public struggle. Accept passive repetition when the student could explain or evaluate.

Check for impact

Students listen to peers because they may be asked to use the response.

The frequency of unprepared 'I don't know' responses decreases.
