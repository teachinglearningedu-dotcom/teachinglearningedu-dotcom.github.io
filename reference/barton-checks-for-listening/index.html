Listening is not the same as understanding

A Check for Listening asks whether pupils attended to and can immediately reproduce what was just said or shown. It is different from a Check for Understanding, which asks pupils to apply, explain or transfer the idea. The distinction matters: during an I Do, the immediate instructional problem may be attention rather than understanding.

Conditions for listening

  • Empty hands and eyes on the teacher where appropriate.
  • Use a stable position for key instructions or explanations (the project summary referenced the “Pastor’s Perch”).
  • Be Seen Looking: visibly scan and wait for full attention.
  • Use a narrated countdown and least-invasive correction to secure 100% attention.
  • Step away from the board or visual when speaking so pupils know whether to look at the teacher or the representation.

Checking the listening

  • 1. Explain or demonstrate a short, carefully bounded idea.
  • 2. Pause and ask pupils to recall or repeat the key point: for example, “What was the question?”, “What did I say the first step was?” or a brief choral response.
  • 3. Use high-frequency cold call or call-and-response so listening is accountable for everyone.
  • 4. Where pupils missed the point, Explain → Frame → Reframe rather than simply repeating a longer version.
  • 5. Only then move to recording, rehearsal or a genuine Check for Understanding.

Silent Teacher

The Explain-phase summary also captured Barton’s “Silent Teacher” idea: explicitly announce a silent demonstration, insist on silence from pupils and use gesture, pointing and visible steps. Because the teacher is not talking, pupils attend closely to movement and often engage in low-stakes self-explanation and prediction. Pauses can be deliberately cued so pupils think about what comes next.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Climate and culture: Securing attention is a precondition for learning; the routine makes attention explicit and accountable.
  • Explain: Checks for Listening sit inside the I Do without turning the explanation into constant hybrid Q&A.
  • Drive thinking for all: Cold call and choral response keep all pupils mentally accountable during teacher explanation.
  • Check and adapt: Listening data tells the teacher whether to reframe the explanation before testing understanding.
