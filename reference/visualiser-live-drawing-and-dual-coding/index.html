Visualiser, live drawing and dual coding

Constructing writing, annotation, diagrams or models live while directing attention and revealing the decisions behind the representation.

What it is: Constructing writing, annotation, diagrams or models live while directing attention and revealing the decisions behind the representation.

Why it helps: Live construction integrates words and visuals, reduces split attention and allows students to see a process unfold. Replication and explanation turn watching into active learning.

Use it when: annotation, diagrams, worked solutions, extended writing and improvement of student work.

Implementation sequence

Prepare the sequence, labels and likely difficult elements in advance.

Begin with a blank page and construct one meaningful element at a time.

Explain or think aloud, then pause speech while students read or copy labels.

Point or trace to direct attention and link new elements to prior knowledge.

Ask students to predict, replicate and explain the next part.

Close the loop with independent reproduction or improvement.

DODON'T
Keep visuals functional and simple. Check sight lines and projection before teaching. Use the visualiser for student examples as well as teacher models. Create an over-detailed artistic diagram that hides the idea. Talk continuously while students are copying. Treat live display as sufficient without student practice.

Check for impact

Students can reproduce and explain the representation independently.

Their attention follows the intended part of the model.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: The Visualiser Guide and Live Drawing; BSCS Explain phase.
