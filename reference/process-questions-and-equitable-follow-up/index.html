Process questions and equitable follow-up

Questions that probe how or why an answer was produced, ask for evidence, comparison or connection, and refine responses for precision.

What it is: Questions that probe how or why an answer was produced, ask for evidence, comparison or connection, and refine responses for precision.

Why it helps: Correctness alone can hide insecure reasoning. Process questions make the method visible, strengthen metacognition and allow the teacher to diagnose the exact point of breakdown.

Use it when: after correct and incorrect answers, during modelling, and whenever transfer depends on understanding the underlying principle.

Implementation sequence

Model the kind of reasoning and vocabulary you expect to hear.

Ask a focused answer question, pause and listen without immediate evaluation.

Follow with how, why, evidence, first-step or comparison prompts.

Ask students to re-articulate with greater precision or in their own words.

Fade sentence stems while retaining the expectation to explain.

DODON'T
Sequence from recall to explanation and application. Ask several students to answer or reformulate the same high-value question. Use elaboration stems temporarily for pupils who need a route into academic speech. Accept the first correct answer and move on automatically. Round up a partial answer by completing the reasoning yourself. Use follow-ups so broad that students cannot identify the required thinking.

Check for impact

Students can name the principle or step that justifies an answer.

Misconceptions become locatable rather than appearing as unexplained wrong answers.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Process Questions, Elaboration Sentence Stems and Equitable Questioning; StepFinder: Add Challenge or Scaffolds Whilst Questioning.
