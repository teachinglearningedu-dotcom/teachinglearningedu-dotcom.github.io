Core routine

Turn and Talk is not simply “discuss with your partner”. The project summary treated it as a tightly structured thinking and rehearsal routine:

  • 1. Pose a question worth discussing.
  • 2. Give individual thinking time first (at least a few seconds; often with a jot or MWB response).
  • 3. Specify who speaks first using an unambiguous role.
  • 4. Partner A explains while Partner B listens.
  • 5. Switch so Partner B responds.
  • 6. End while discussion is still lively — at the “crest of the wave”.
  • 7. Harvest thinking through Cold Call, a re-vote, or a prompt that rewards listening rather than only speaking.

Make sure there is something to discuss

Turn and Talk is particularly productive when pupils have different answers, need to justify a method, or must compare reasoning. Useful prompts include: “Convince your partner”, “If you agree, explain why someone might disagree”, “Whose answer changed?”, or “What did your partner say that was interesting?”

Responsive-teaching uses

  • After a range of MWB / ABCD answers, especially when roughly 40–80% are correct.
  • After an all-hands-up or vote where the class is divided.
  • When no one initially volunteers: individual think → talk → Cold Call.
  • After an I Do to rehearse the explanation before We Do.
  • Inside 4–2 independent-practice cycles: four minutes silent work, two minutes paired discussion, then return to independent work.

Accountability

The pair should be accountable for the quality of talk and listening. The project notes stressed that “helping others is the hardest job”: praise and correction can be directed at pairs, and the final Cold Call can ask a pupil to report their partner’s thinking.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Drive thinking for all: Structures independent thinking, collaborative refinement and accountability.
  • Check and adapt: Useful when responses are mixed; discussion can be followed by a re-vote and teacher explanation.
  • Explain: Can provide rehearsal after an I Do without co-constructing the original explanation.
  • Secure learning through practice: The 4–2 cycle uses partner discussion to support sustained independent practice without turning the whole task collaborative.
