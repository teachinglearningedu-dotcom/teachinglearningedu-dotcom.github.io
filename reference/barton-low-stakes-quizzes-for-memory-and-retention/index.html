Core idea

The low-stakes quiz is a planned, substantial retrieval event designed to strengthen long-term memory. It is not simply a miniature test or a score-producing assessment. The project summary described a typical model of one weekly mixed-topic quiz of around ten questions, aiming for an average success rate of roughly 80%.

Routine

  • 1. Pupils retrieve answers independently and in silence.
  • 2. Before answers are revealed, pupils can record confidence in each response. This distinguishes a lucky correct answer from secure knowledge and, importantly, highlights high-confidence errors.
  • 3. Reveal complete worked solutions rather than only final answers.
  • 4. Prioritise review around high-confidence errors and important gaps.
  • 5. Create Review Cards or another personalised retrieval mechanism for the items that need revisiting.
  • 6. Schedule those items for future retrieval rather than treating correction as the endpoint.

Scores and stakes

The project summary generally advised against routinely recording quiz scores. The purpose is learning and memory: pupils should attend to what they know, what they misremember and what needs to be revisited, rather than optimising for a mark.

Why confidence matters

A high-confidence wrong answer is especially valuable diagnostic information because it points to knowledge that may be strongly encoded but incorrect. Those errors deserve priority in subsequent review and retrieval.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Do Now: The same retrieval principles can inform Do Now design, although the LSQ is a larger, less frequent retrieval event.
  • Lesson preparation: Memory and retention are deliberately scheduled across time rather than left to ad hoc revision.
  • Check and adapt: Confidence plus correctness reveals which knowledge needs re-teaching or planned retrieval.
  • Feedback to improve: Worked solutions and Review Cards convert quiz evidence into action rather than a mark.
