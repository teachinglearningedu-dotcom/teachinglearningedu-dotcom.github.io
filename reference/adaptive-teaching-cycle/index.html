Anticipate barriers

Start from what pupils need to learn. Identify prerequisite knowledge, likely misconceptions and barriers to access before the lesson. Plan a diagnostic question and the smallest useful adjustment. Keep the curricular goal ambitious.

Use assessment formatively

Choose a response method that reveals the knowledge you need to inspect. For a whole-class decision, collect independent responses from everyone where possible. A correct answer, completed work or confident delivery is not sufficient on its own to establish understanding.

Respond and adapt

Interpret the cause of an error before choosing the response. A knowledge gap may need explanation and guided practice. A misconception may need a contrasting example and a fresh diagnostic question. An access barrier may need a temporary scaffold or a different representation.

Monitor and adjust

After changing the teaching, use a fresh question or task to check whether the adjustment helped. Revisit the learning later and reduce support when pupils can succeed independently.

A practical planning sequence

Write the intended learning, the likely barrier, the diagnostic prompt, the response you would make and the follow-up check. Choose one change that you can rehearse and observe.

Participation and motivation

Make errors safe to discuss. Give pupils a genuine chance to prepare an answer. Describe successful strategies and improvement precisely, and choose tasks that provide a challenging but achievable route to success.
