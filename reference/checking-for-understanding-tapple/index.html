Checking for understanding / TAPPLE

A continuous feedback loop in which teachers ask, pause, select a response method, listen or look, interpret the evidence and act.

What it is: A continuous feedback loop in which teachers ask, pause, select a response method, listen or look, interpret the evidence and act.

Why it helps: Learning is invisible unless elicited. High-quality checks replace unreliable perceptions with evidence and make teaching responsive rather than plan-driven.

Use it when: before teaching prerequisites, after each small input, at a lesson hinge and before independent practice.

Implementation sequence

Identify the newest or most important knowledge and likely misconception.

Ask a diagnostic prompt without leaking the answer through hints.

Pause long enough for everyone to think.

Pick an all-student response method where feasible; otherwise sample indicator students.

Interpret the pattern and decide whether to continue, clarify, model, practise or extend.

Recheck after the response so the loop is closed.

DODON'T
Ask 'What have you understood about...?' rather than 'Have you understood?'. Use critique, error analysis, recall and explanation questions. Make any pre-check hint avoidable or delay it until after commitment. Use 'Any questions?' as the primary check. Treat volunteers, compliance or completed work as a representative sample. Collect answers without changing the next instructional decision.

Check for impact

The teacher can state what evidence prompted the adaptation.

A fresh question confirms that the identified misconception has reduced.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: TAPPLE and Check for Student Understanding; Tips for Teachers: Check for Understanding.
