Circulation and actionable live feedback

Purposeful movement through a planned route to scan, read, compare work with criteria and give brief feedback that prompts immediate improvement.

What it is: Purposeful movement through a planned route to scan, read, compare work with criteria and give brief feedback that prompts immediate improvement.

Why it helps: Early systematic observation exposes misconceptions before they spread. Prompt-clue-direct feedback keeps students doing the cognitive work while allowing the teacher to support many pupils efficiently.

Use it when: guided and independent practice, especially where layout, method or written evidence can be scanned quickly.

Implementation sequence

Plan a route, success criteria, must-check students and likely errors.

Move systematically and use a brief scan-read-compare diagnostic.

Record exemplars, partial responses and misconceptions with quick notation.

Use prompt, then clue, then a precise direction only if needed.

Mark the exact location and bound the improvement task.

Return to check that the action was completed correctly.

DODON'T
Break the plane early and maintain awareness of the whole room. Keep language economical and actionable. Use the evidence to decide whether feedback should become whole-class reteaching. Zig-zag only towards raised hands. Explain at length to one student while the rest become invisible. Give feedback without returning to close the loop.

Check for impact

All parts of the room and priority students are sampled.

Students make the requested improvement while the thinking is still live.
