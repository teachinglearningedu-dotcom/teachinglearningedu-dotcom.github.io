Signal for attention and habits of attention

A consistent cue followed by observable, inclusive attention behaviours that prepare students to listen, look or think about the intended content.

What it is: A consistent cue followed by observable, inclusive attention behaviours that prepare students to listen, look or think about the intended content.

Why it helps: Attention is the gateway to learning. A rehearsed signal reduces transition time and ambiguity, while habits such as empty hands, orienting to the speaker and active listening protect cognitive bandwidth.

Use it when: before explanations, instructions, peer contributions, demonstrations and any transition from activity to shared attention.

Implementation sequence

Select one calm auditory or visual signal and explain its purpose.

Define what students should do when the signal occurs using observable directions.

Model, rehearse and time the routine until it becomes fluent.

Signal, pause, scan the full room and use least-intrusive corrections.

Begin only when the agreed standard is met; adapt the form for legitimate sensory or neurological needs.

DODON'T
Use the same signal consistently across lessons. Separate securing attention from checking that instructions were understood. Praise rapid, complete transitions with precise narration. Repeat or raise the volume of instructions over partial attention. Treat eye contact as the only valid sign of attention. Use a theatrical cue that is harder to sustain than the routine itself.

Check for impact

The class moves to the agreed attention posture quickly and without repeated prompts.

Students can recall and act on the instruction given immediately after the signal.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Signal for Attention, Habits of Attention and Securing Attention; Tips for Teachers: Checks for Listening.
