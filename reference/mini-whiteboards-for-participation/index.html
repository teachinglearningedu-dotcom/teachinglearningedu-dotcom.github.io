Mini-whiteboards for participation

A routine in which every student commits a short written, symbolic or multiple-choice response and reveals it on a common cue.

What it is: A routine in which every student commits a short written, symbolic or multiple-choice response and reveals it on a common cue.

Why it helps: Simultaneous response raises participation, lowers the permanence of error and gives every learner a reason to think. It also prepares students for later explanation and discussion.

Use it when: short answers, calculations, diagrams, vocabulary, multiple-choice reasoning and book-to-board transfer during independent practice.

Implementation sequence

Teach equipment, silence, no-doodling, writing and reveal routines explicitly.

Standardise the expected response so boards can be scanned reliably.

Give at least 30 seconds or suitable think time before the show cue.

Use both sides or staged reveals to preserve independence and flow.

Select responses for cold call, comparison or whole-class discussion.

DODON'T
Use concise prompts and consistent 'show me' cues. Scan priority students and every part of the room. Use book-to-board to make final answers visible during practice. Use boards for long extended responses better suited to books. Allow copying by letting answers appear at different times without control. Collect data and move on without feedback or adaptation.

Check for impact

Every student commits an answer before seeing peers' responses.

Participation remains high and routines consume little lesson time.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Mini-Whiteboards; Tips for Teachers: Mini Whiteboards.
