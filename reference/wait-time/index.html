Wait time

A deliberate pause after a question and a second pause after a student's response before the teacher evaluates or moves on.

What it is: A deliberate pause after a question and a second pause after a student's response before the teacher evaluates or moves on.

Why it helps: Thinking, retrieval and formulation take time. A pause increases the number of students able to prepare and improves the likelihood of reasoned rather than impulsive responses.

Use it when: questions requiring explanation, inference, calculation, recall from longer-term memory or use of precise vocabulary.

Implementation sequence

Explain why silence will follow questions and normalise it as thinking time.

Ask the question before choosing a respondent.

Pause for roughly 3-5 seconds or longer where complexity demands it.

Maintain neutral scanning without signalling the eventual respondent.

After the answer, pause again so the speaker can extend and peers can process.

DODON'T
Display complex questions so transient wording remains available. Use writing when the cognitive demand exceeds what can be held mentally. Combine wait time with cold call and follow-up questions. Fill the pause by rephrasing, hinting or answering your own question. Apply the same fixed pause to every question regardless of complexity. Interpret a fast confident response as proof of understanding.

Check for impact

More students can offer a prepared answer when sampled.

Answers contain more reasoning, evidence and subject vocabulary.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Wait Time; Tips for Teachers: Check for Understanding and Cold Call.
