Whole-class feedback and Show Call

Reviewing a set of work to identify patterns, then teaching from common strengths, misconceptions and strategically selected examples rather than writing repeated individual comments.

What it is: Reviewing a set of work to identify patterns, then teaching from common strengths, misconceptions and strategically selected examples rather than writing repeated individual comments.

Why it helps: Pattern-based feedback is efficient and can make quality visible to everyone. Its impact comes from modelling, comparison and student action, not from the feedback sheet itself.

Use it when: when several students share a misconception, success feature or next step, and when examples can improve collective understanding.

Implementation sequence

Review all or a representative set of work against the same criteria.

Record common strengths, misconceptions, exemplars and next actions.

Set a courteous, safe culture before displaying student work.

Make responses visible and compare, annotate or improve them live.

Require students to correct, redraft, rehearse or retry.

Re-teach and recheck where the error is widespread.

DODON'T
Select examples for their instructional value, not only because they are perfect. Use tick-with-me or annotation so students track the feedback actively. Highlight changed thinking and why it changed. Use whole-class feedback to avoid diagnosing individual work. Publicly expose a student or invite ridicule. Deliver a feedback monologue without improvement time.

Check for impact

Every student can identify and apply the relevant action to their own work.

A follow-up task shows improvement in the common gap.
