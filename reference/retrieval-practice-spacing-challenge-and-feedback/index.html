Retrieval practice: spacing, challenge and feedback

Effortfully recalling previously taught knowledge after some forgetting, with low stakes and feedback that makes the retrieval event itself part of learning.

What it is: Effortfully recalling previously taught knowledge after some forgetting, with low stakes and feedback that makes the retrieval event itself part of learning.

Why it helps: Retrieval strengthens future retrievability. Spacing and appropriate difficulty create desirable challenge, while feedback prevents repeated recall of an error.

Use it when: Do Nows, quizzes, end-of-lesson review, homework follow-up and cumulative practice across a curriculum.

Implementation sequence

Map key knowledge across increasing intervals rather than relying on recent content.

Mix accessible and challenging items so students experience effort without routine failure.

Require recall before notes or cues are made available.

Provide an accurate answer and brief corrective explanation.

Schedule another retrieval opportunity, especially for weak items.

DODON'T
Teach students how to self-quiz and mark accurately. Connect each item to curriculum knowledge rather than trivia. Keep stakes low enough that error remains informative. Use massed repetition or cramming as the main retrieval plan. Make every quiz a graded performance event. Assume a correct immediate answer means durable learning.

Check for impact

Knowledge remains accessible after longer intervals and in varied contexts.

Students can identify and revisit specific retrieval gaps.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Retrieval Practice - Seven Essential Principles; StepFinder: Ensure Consolidation and Retrieval.
