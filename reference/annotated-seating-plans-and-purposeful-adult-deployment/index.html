Annotated seating plans and purposeful adult deployment

Using current knowledge of pupils to position students and adults deliberately, annotate practical supports, plan talk partners and define circulation or check routes.

What it is: Using current knowledge of pupils to position students and adults deliberately, annotate practical supports, plan talk partners and define circulation or check routes.

Why it helps: Seating and adult roles influence attention, participation, visibility and independence. Proactive planning reduces reactive intervention while avoiding dependency on a teaching assistant.

Use it when: every class, particularly where attendance, talk partners, sensory needs, literacy barriers or adult support materially affect access.

Implementation sequence

Maintain an up-to-date plan that matches the actual room layout.

Annotate concise, strategy-focused information rather than sensitive detail or labels alone.

Plan purposeful pairs, temporary triads and adjustments for absence.

Mark teacher and, where relevant, teaching-assistant circulation and check routes.

Brief additional adults on the learning goal, vocabulary, misconceptions, prompts and feedback to gather.

Review the plan when needs, relationships or response evidence change.

DODON'T
Use annotations such as 'pre-warn before cold call' or 'check written instruction understood'. Keep the teacher connected to pupils with SEND and lower prior attainment. Use adults to promote independence through prompts, clues and models. Group students routinely by label or perceived ability. Make the teaching assistant the default teacher for pupils with SEND. Leave sensitive annotations visible to pupils or visitors.

Check for impact

All pupils have a viable talk partner and access to teacher attention.

Support results in greater independence rather than permanent adult dependence.

Sources informing this profile: BSCS Policy: Seating Plans and Appendix 5; StepFinder: Seating Plan and Classroom Layout strategies.
