Everyone, Together, Anyone (ETA) and active participation

A participation framework that moves between responses from everyone, structured thinking with a partner and individual accountability.

What it is: A participation framework that moves between responses from everyone, structured thinking with a partner and individual accountability.

Why it helps: Different modes reveal different evidence. Whole-class responses create universal commitment, paired talk rehearses and refines ideas, and individual questioning probes reasoning while keeping everyone prepared.

Use it when: planning a sequence of checks, explanations or discussion in which participation would otherwise be left to chance.

Implementation sequence

Identify the thinking required: recall, application, explanation, evaluation or revision.

Use Everyone methods such as mini-whiteboards, writing or choral response for universal commitment.

Use Together methods for rehearsal, comparison and elaboration.

Use Anyone methods such as cold call to sample and probe individual reasoning.

Respond to the evidence and vary the mode rather than repeating it mechanically.

DODON'T
Front-load how students will respond. Preserve an individual product before collaboration. Use the sequence flexibly rather than treating it as a fixed three-step formula. Assume visible busyness means students are thinking about the content. Use paired talk as a break from teacher talk without a question worth discussing. Collect responses without adapting instruction.

Check for impact

Every student produces repeated visible or audible evidence of thinking.

The teacher's sampling becomes more representative of the class.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Everyone, Together, Anyone and Active Participation; BSCS Policy: Participation and Thinking Ratio.
