Core principle

Responsive teaching means changing what you do because of evidence about what pupils currently understand. A check is only useful if it can lead to a decision. The project summary therefore organised checking around specific decision points and response thresholds.

Five high-value CFU points in a lesson

  • 1. Do Now review.
  • 2. Prerequisite knowledge before teaching the new idea.
  • 3. The basics of the new idea after explanation.
  • 4. One or more hinge questions during the learning episode.
  • 5. Access to independent practice: are pupils ready to work successfully without the same level of support?

Gather reliable evidence

  • Avoid volunteers as the main source of data.
  • Cold Call gives a sample but can be incomplete or contaminated by prior answers.
  • ABCD can be efficient when the diagnostic question is well designed.
  • Circulation can be rich but takes time.
  • Mini whiteboards were the preferred whole-class method in the project summary for many decisions.
  • Do not reveal the answer before you have gathered the evidence.

Decision thresholds

EvidenceInterpretationResponse from project summary
≈80%+ correctNearly everyone is secureBriefly reveal/model, keeping the final answer until the end; record the small number who need checking later; move on or use a “What if?” variation.
≈40–80% correctSome are secure, some are notMake responses visible; use warm call / Turn and Talk; re-vote; ask who switched and why; explain succinctly; ask a planned similar follow-up question.
<40% correctFew are secureRegain attention; re-explain or re-model in a different way; use Explain–Frame–Reframe and Checks for Listening; then ask a fresh follow-up CFU before continuing.

Worked example from the project summary

  • Do Now Q1: 28/30 correct → quick model, note the two pupils, move on.
  • Do Now Q2: 17/30 correct → display three different answers, Turn and Talk, re-vote, teacher explanation, then a planned similar follow-up question.

Explain after the check

When an explanation is needed, keep it concise enough that all pupils can explain it back. Focus on one thing at a time and use a concrete example. Pupils who were initially correct still need to attend: the teacher is building a shared, precise explanation, not only fixing the pupils who were wrong.

Revisit later

For the 40–80% and <40% pathways, the project summary also planned delayed checking through later retrieval, Do Nows, homework or a low-stakes quiz — sometimes one or two weeks later. Immediate success is not treated as proof of durable learning.

DoAvoid

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Check and adapt: This is the direct operational model: gather evidence, interpret it, adapt, then re-check.
  • Do Now: The Do Now review is one of the five planned CFU points.
  • Explain: Prerequisite checks and post-explanation basics checks determine what to explain and whether to re-explain.
  • Secure learning through practice: The final checkpoint asks whether pupils have enough security to enter independent practice.
  • Drive thinking for all: Reliable CFU depends on high-participation response methods, especially MWBs.
