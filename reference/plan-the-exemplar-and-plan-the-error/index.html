Plan the exemplar and plan the error

Preparing a high-quality model response and the misconceptions, weak examples or incorrect processes most likely to occur.

What it is: Preparing a high-quality model response and the misconceptions, weak examples or incorrect processes most likely to occur.

Why it helps: An exemplar clarifies the destination; planned errors help the teacher diagnose thinking and design explanations or distractors that address causes rather than symptoms.

Use it when: high-leverage questions, extended responses, complex procedures and tasks with recurring misconceptions.

Implementation sequence

Complete the task yourself in the form students are expected to produce.

Annotate the essential success features, vocabulary, decisions and presentation.

List likely errors and classify them as knowledge, attention, process, fluency or self-regulation gaps.

Create a near-miss response or distractor for each important misconception.

Plan how students will compare, diagnose and improve examples before revising their own work.

DODON'T
Select only the features students can realistically apply next. Use contrasting examples to make quality and misconception boundaries visible. Keep the final correct record available after analysis. Present a polished model without revealing how it was produced. Use obviously absurd wrong answers that reveal nothing about thinking. Ask students to improve work without time or a precise action.

Check for impact

Students can identify and reproduce the essential success features.

The teacher's planned distractors correspond to actual response patterns.

Sources informing this profile: BSCS Policy Appendix 1; StepFinder: Exemplar Planning, Plan for Likely Errors and Hinge Questions.
