Cold calling

Asking the question to the whole class, allowing thinking time and then warmly inviting a selected student to respond.

What it is: Asking the question to the whole class, allowing thinking time and then warmly inviting a selected student to respond.

Why it helps: Pause-then-name keeps every student accountable for thinking and produces a more representative sample than relying on volunteers. Frequent, supportive use reduces stigma and builds a culture of contribution.

Use it when: across lesson phases for recall, explanation, comparison, listening checks and sampling longer written responses.

Implementation sequence

Explain that cold call is an invitation and a way to include everyone, not to catch students out.

Secure attention, ask the question to all and give at least several seconds or appropriate writing time.

Scan thinking, then select strategically or randomly according to the purpose of the check.

Invite the response warmly; support volume and precision.

Probe, sequence several answers or return after paired/written rehearsal.

DODON'T
Use question-pause-name rather than name-question. Mix direct cold call with pre-call, paired rehearsal and written preparation. Use indicator students when their response would reveal an important gap. Use cold call only for behaviour management or when someone is inattentive. Select a student before giving the rest of the class a reason to think. Abandon the student after 'I don't know' without a second chance.

Check for impact

More students prepare answers even when they are not ultimately called.

Responses become fuller and participation is distributed more equitably.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Cold Calling and Equitable Questioning; Tips for Teachers: Cold Call.
