Fading support and backward fading

Reducing a model or scaffold in small evidence-led steps, often removing the final step first while keeping the task and method stable.

What it is: Reducing a model or scaffold in small evidence-led steps, often removing the final step first while keeping the task and method stable.

Why it helps: Large jumps from full model to blank page overload novices. Backward fading preserves the start of the process and transfers responsibility gradually until the full sequence is independent.

Use it when: multi-step procedures and extended responses after students have seen and understood a complete model.

Implementation sequence

Show a complete example and explain why each step matters.

Keep the model visible for the first guided attempt.

Remove the final step and ask students to complete it.

Remove additional steps gradually while keeping the task type stable.

Use checks to decide whether to fade, hold or temporarily restore support.

Confirm learning through a fully independent similar task.

DODON'T
Fade based on competence rather than elapsed time. Ask students to justify visible and missing steps. Allow a progression to span multiple lessons. Remove the opening steps first and leave students unsure how to begin. Change the method and reduce support at the same time. Continue fading when widespread errors show students are not ready.

Check for impact

Accuracy remains stable as each support is removed.

Students can initiate and complete the process independently.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Fading Support; StepFinder: Use Backward Fading and Fade Scaffolds.
