Do Now: retrieval-rich lesson start

A short, immediately accessible and independently completed start-of-lesson routine, usually built around spaced retrieval rather than new content.

What it is: A short, immediately accessible and independently completed start-of-lesson routine, usually built around spaced retrieval rather than new content.

Why it helps: The routine creates a purposeful start and uses retrieval to strengthen future access to previously taught knowledge. An achievable sequence also builds early success and gives the teacher useful response data.

Use it when: the start of lessons, with a separate prerequisite check later if new learning depends on particular knowledge.

Implementation sequence

Display about four clear, single-answer questions plus an optional challenge in a consistent place.

Sequence from an accessible first item to a more challenging final item, aiming for roughly three out of four correct.

Use a visible timer: approximately five minutes to complete and five minutes to review.

Circulate twice: first for routine and participation, then for response patterns and misconceptions.

Review responsively using one-question-at-a-time checks, book-to-board whiteboards, cold call or choral response.

Let latecomers join the current task and release one question at a time if focus is weak.

DODON'T
Use spaced content from previous lessons and topics. Prepare concise models and follow-up items for likely errors. Move quickly when most students are correct and follow up the few later. Use the Do Now mainly to test prerequisites for today's lesson. Use ambiguous, trick or multiple-answer prompts. Allow review to become a lengthy reteaching episode.

Check for impact

Students begin independently with minimal teacher direction.

Retrieval accuracy improves across later spaced appearances.

Sources informing this profile: Tips for Teachers: Do Now; DistilED: The Do Now Checklist and Retrieval Practice.
