Structured peer feedback

Students use clear criteria and modelled language to identify a specific strength and an actionable improvement in a peer's work.

What it is: Students use clear criteria and modelled language to identify a specific strength and an actionable improvement in a peer's work.

Why it helps: Giving feedback requires students to internalise quality and compare evidence with criteria. The receiver benefits only when suggestions are accurate, kind, specific and followed by action.

Use it when: after students understand the content and success criteria well enough to evaluate work reliably.

Implementation sequence

Model the feedback process using an anonymous or teacher-created example.

Provide a small number of precise success criteria and feedback stems.

Require observation and evidence before a suggestion.

Make feedback specific, referenced, actionable, prescriptive and kind.

Give the receiver time to clarify and improve the work.

Sample feedback quality as well as the resulting revision.

DODON'T
Train students to distinguish praise from useful evidence. Use paired roles and a clear time limit. Check that feedback is technically accurate before action. Use peer feedback before students know what quality looks like. Ask for unrestricted 'two stars and a wish' comments. End after comments are exchanged without revision.

Check for impact

Feedback refers accurately to criteria and names a feasible action.

Revised work improves in the targeted feature.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Peer Feedback; StepFinder: Build a Culture of Feedback.
