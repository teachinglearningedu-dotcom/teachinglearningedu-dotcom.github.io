Explicit vocabulary instruction with active practice

Teaching a word's meaning, pronunciation, form, examples, non-examples and connections, followed by repeated retrieval and use in context.

What it is: Teaching a word's meaning, pronunciation, form, examples, non-examples and connections, followed by repeated retrieval and use in context.

Why it helps: Vocabulary is both knowledge and a tool for thinking. Multiple encounters and active production make words retrievable and reduce language as a barrier to curriculum access.

Use it when: high-utility academic language, subject-specific terms and words whose everyday meaning differs from their disciplinary meaning.

Implementation sequence

Select a manageable number of essential words and prepare student-friendly definitions.

Model pronunciation and morphology; use choral rehearsal where appropriate.

Show the word in examples, non-examples and a meaningful visual or sentence.

Require students to use it through writing, paired talk and cold call.

Check for misconceptions and compare or connect related words.

Revisit through exit tickets and spaced retrieval.

DODON'T
Keep definitions precise and consistent across resources. Provide sentence stems while students first use the word. Correct pronunciation and meaning immediately but supportively. Give a long word list without planned use. Rely on copying definitions as the learning event. Treat one correct use as secure long-term knowledge.

Check for impact

Students use the word accurately in new contexts.

Later retrieval is fluent without the original scaffold.

Sources informing this profile: StepFinder: Teach New Vocabulary with Active Practice; DistilED: Choral Response and Non-Examples.
