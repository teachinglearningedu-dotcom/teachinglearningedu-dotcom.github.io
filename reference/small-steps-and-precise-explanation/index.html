Small steps and precise explanation

Breaking complex content into coherent, cumulative units and explaining each with concise, accurate language before immediate guided practice.

What it is: Breaking complex content into coherent, cumulative units and explaining each with concise, accurate language before immediate guided practice.

Why it helps: Working memory is limited, especially for novices. Small steps reduce interacting elements and create repeated chances to check and correct before complexity accumulates.

Use it when: procedures, dense concepts, new vocabulary and tasks where one missed step prevents later success.

Implementation sequence

Define one micro-goal and link it to secure prior knowledge.

Explain and model the first step, including why it works.

Give every student an immediate attempt and check the response.

Correct or reteach before adding the next step.

Combine steps gradually and fade prompts once the sequence is secure.

DODON'T
Use consistent terminology and repeated structures. Anticipate the misconception at each step. Move on when roughly 80% are secure, then follow up the few. Fragment content so much that the overall structure disappears. Add several dependent steps without a check. Confuse brisk pace with rapid teacher speech.

Check for impact

Students succeed on a similar item immediately after each modelled step.

They can explain the reason for the step, not just imitate it.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Small Steps and Explaining with Precision; StepFinder: Give Clear and Memorable Explanations.
