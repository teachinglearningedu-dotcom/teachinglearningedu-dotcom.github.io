Core idea

Mini whiteboards maximise participation and give the teacher rapid, visible evidence from all pupils. Their value depends on a tight routine: everyone thinks, everyone commits, everyone reveals at the same moment, and the teacher scans for patterns before deciding what to do next.

Core routine

  • 1. Ask a question that can be answered clearly on a board.
  • 2. Give genuine thinking time; the project summary used around 30 seconds where appropriate rather than rushing pupils to display.
  • 3. Pupils write while keeping boards private.
  • 4. Use a common reveal cue: “Boards up in 3, 2, 1.”
  • 5. Scan the whole class, prioritising patterns, misconceptions and indicator pupils rather than commenting on the first board you notice.
  • 6. Decide whether to move on, model, discuss, re-teach or ask a follow-up question.

Reliable data

The board routine should prevent copying and answer contamination. Standardised positioning and simultaneous reveal matter because the purpose is not merely participation; it is trustworthy evidence of individual thinking.

Useful applications from the project summaries

  • Book-to-Board checking during Do Now review.
  • Step-by-step We Do, where each procedural step is completed and checked before the next.
  • ABCD / diagnostic responses.
  • Follow-up re-checks after a teacher explanation.
  • Independent-practice interventions: “Show me how you got that.”

Do / avoid

DoAvoid
Teach one consistent reveal routine.Letting pupils hold boards up as soon as they finish.
Protect thinking time before boards go up.Revealing or strongly cueing the answer before the scan.
Scan the class before revealing the correct answer.Using MWBs for lengthy responses that are difficult to scan reliably.
Use what you see to adapt immediately.Commentating on individual boards before you have read the class pattern.
Ask questions whose answers can be interpreted quickly.Treating “boards used” as evidence that checking was effective.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Drive thinking for all: Every pupil must generate a response, raising participation and accountability.
  • Check and adapt: MWBs are the project’s preferred method for rapid whole-class evidence in many responsive-teaching moments.
  • Do Now: Book-to-Board and all-MWB routines can make review efficient and responsive.
  • Secure learning through practice: Step-by-step boards are a strong We Do structure before pupils work independently.
