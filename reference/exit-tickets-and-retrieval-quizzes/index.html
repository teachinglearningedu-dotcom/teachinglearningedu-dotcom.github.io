Exit tickets and retrieval quizzes

Brief independent end-of-lesson or cumulative questions designed for rapid analysis and a clear instructional follow-up.

What it is: Brief independent end-of-lesson or cumulative questions designed for rapid analysis and a clear instructional follow-up.

Why it helps: Independent responses provide cleaner evidence than self-report and allow the next lesson to begin from actual gaps. Retrieval quizzes also consolidate and expose what requires further practice.

Use it when: when a small set of core knowledge or misconceptions should shape the next lesson or revision plan.

Implementation sequence

Choose a few questions focused tightly on essential learning.

Include likely misconceptions and formats that can be analysed quickly.

Set a threshold for success before administering the check.

Ensure independent completion without help or peer checking.

Divide responses by success, dig into the important gaps and decide the next action.

Link gaps to revision material and future retrieval.

DODON'T
Sample deliberately if time prevents reviewing every response immediately. Prepare model answers and select one or two items for deeper feedback. Use the results visibly in the next lesson. Ask so many questions that analysis becomes unmanageable. Collect tickets without a planned response. Treat the ticket as a high-stakes grade rather than learning evidence.

Check for impact

The next lesson or homework directly responds to the diagnosed pattern.

Students can identify and retrieve the corrected knowledge later.

Sources informing this profile: StepFinder: Use Exit Tickets and Retrieval Quizzes; BSCS Feedback and Homework Principles.
