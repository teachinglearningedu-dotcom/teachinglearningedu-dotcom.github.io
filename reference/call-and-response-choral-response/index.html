Call and response / Choral response

A clearly cued routine in which the whole class gives the same concise response in unison.

What it is: A clearly cued routine in which the whole class gives the same concise response in unison.

Why it helps: It multiplies opportunities to retrieve and rehearse, supports pronunciation and fluency, and establishes collective participation. Its reliability depends on a single clear answer and disciplined cueing.

Use it when: short factual recall, definitions, formulae, pronunciation, technical vocabulary, routines and key statements students need to remember accurately.

Implementation sequence

Teach the cue and model the expected timing, volume and unison response.

Explain or ask the prompt, then allow thinking time before the cue.

Use a consistent signal such as 'Together in 3, 2, 1'.

Listen for participation and accuracy; repeat to improve precision where necessary.

Follow with individual or written checks when independent understanding matters.

DODON'T
Use Explain-Frame-Reframe to check both directions of an important relationship. Cold call first, then bring the accurate response together chorally. Push for clear volume and full participation without shaming. Use choral response for open questions with several defensible answers. Assume a loud unison answer proves every student understands. Cue so quickly that students merely echo rather than retrieve.

Check for impact

All students can later produce the knowledge independently.

Technical vocabulary is pronounced and used accurately.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Choral Response; Tips for Teachers: Call and Respond.
