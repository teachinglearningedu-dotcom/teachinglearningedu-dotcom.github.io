Worked examples and providing models

Showing a complete or partially completed solution or product, with the structure, sequence and decisions made explicit before students perform independently.

What it is: Showing a complete or partially completed solution or product, with the structure, sequence and decisions made explicit before students perform independently.

Why it helps: Novices can focus on the schema of the task rather than using working memory for unguided search. Multiple and faded examples create a bridge from observation to performance.

Use it when: multi-step procedures, extended writing, practical methods, diagrams and unfamiliar task formats.

Implementation sequence

Diagnose prerequisites and define the success criteria.

Present a complete example and reveal why each step or feature is present.

Ask students to identify, explain or compare key decisions.

Guide a similar example step by step, eliciting contributions from everyone.

Use completion problems or backward fading before independent application.

Return to the model when errors reveal an insecure step.

DODON'T
Model at normal speed, then repeat slowly in chunks. Use strong, weak and near-miss examples for comparison. Keep the task stable while reducing support. Display a finished model without making the process visible. Ask novices to discover the method through trial and error. Jump directly from full model to a substantially different independent task.

Check for impact

Students can name and reproduce the process on a similar task.

Support can be removed without a collapse in accuracy.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Worked Examples and Provide Models; StepFinder: Use Modelling as a Scaffold.
