Positive narration, precise praise and motivation

Reinforcing the actions, effort, quality and norms that support learning while building belonging, competence and value.

What it is: Reinforcing the actions, effort, quality and norms that support learning while building belonging, competence and value.

Why it helps: Students are more likely to invest attention when success feels attainable and the task feels worthwhile. Describing effective behaviour also gives peers a concrete model without interrupting learning.

Use it when: after directions, during independent practice, at moments of persistence and when establishing a new classroom norm.

Implementation sequence

Position and scan deliberately for the behaviour or work quality you want to reinforce.

Name the observable action and, where useful, connect it to learning.

Use non-verbal acknowledgement for low-key reinforcement.

Narrate effort, revision and successful strategy use rather than fixed traits.

Follow up important work so students see that it has value.

DODON'T
Keep praise authentic, specific and proportionate. Engineer early success before asking for greater persistence. Build relationships outside correction moments. Use generic praise that gives no information about what worked. Praise speed when accuracy or reasoning matters more. Use public praise in a way that embarrasses an individual.

Check for impact

Students repeat the reinforced behaviour without escalating rewards.

Participation and persistence broaden beyond the initially confident pupils.

Sources informing this profile: StepFinder: Motivate Students to Learn and Reinforce Positive Behaviours; DistilED: TCMH.
