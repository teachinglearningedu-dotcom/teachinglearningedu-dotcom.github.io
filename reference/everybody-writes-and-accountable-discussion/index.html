Everybody Writes and accountable discussion

Using brief individual writing before or after talk so every student develops, records and revises an idea.

What it is: Using brief individual writing before or after talk so every student develops, records and revises an idea.

Why it helps: Writing slows thought, preserves an independent response and gives less confident speakers something to contribute. Post-discussion revision converts talk into a durable learning product.

Use it when: open questions, text analysis, comparison of methods, debate and feedback on student responses.

Implementation sequence

Set a tightly specified writing prompt and short time limit.

Circulate to identify ideas and select students for discussion.

Use paired or whole-class discussion to compare, challenge and refine.

Require students to revise, summarise or improve the original writing.

Sample the final product to check whether discussion moved thinking forward.

DODON'T
Front the writing before discussion when independent thinking matters. Make the final written product explicit. Use displayed responses to compare quality and reasoning. Treat copying notes as evidence of thought. Let discussion replace an individual record of learning. Ask for lengthy writing when a short commitment would suffice.

Check for impact

All students have a response that becomes more accurate or developed.

Discussion draws on visible ideas rather than improvisation by a few.

Sources informing this profile: StepFinder/TLAC: Everybody Writes, Front the Writing and Building Ratio Through Discussion.
