Solid entry routines and threshold

A short, explicitly taught sequence for moving from corridor to classroom and beginning independent learning immediately.

What it is: A short, explicitly taught sequence for moving from corridor to classroom and beginning independent learning immediately.

Why it helps: Predictability removes logistical uncertainty, protects learning time and secures attention before working memory is occupied by avoidable transitions.

Use it when: the start of every lesson and whenever entry has become slow, noisy or dependent on repeated teacher reminders.

Implementation sequence

Prime students at the threshold and greet them with calm warmth.

State a small number of observable entry expectations.

Control the flow, direct students to assigned seats and make the Do Now immediately accessible.

Scan, use non-verbal cues and positively narrate successful entry.

Rehearse or reset the routine when the standard is missed.

DODON'T
Keep materials and starter location consistent. Build brief positive interactions without interrupting the routine. Use Do It Again proportionately to secure the standard. Begin giving academic instructions while students are still entering. Change the entry procedure from lesson to lesson. Use a whole-class reset when a quiet individual correction would suffice.

Check for impact

Students begin the Do Now without prompting and within the expected time.

Teacher attention is available for relationships and diagnosis rather than logistics.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Solid Entry Routines; StepFinder: Establish an Entry Routine; BSCS Policy: Threshold and Do Now.
