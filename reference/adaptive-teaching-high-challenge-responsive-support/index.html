Adaptive teaching: high challenge, responsive support

Maintaining a shared ambitious goal while adjusting explanation, representation, scaffolding, grouping, time or practice in response to barriers and live evidence.

What it is: Maintaining a shared ambitious goal while adjusting explanation, representation, scaffolding, grouping, time or practice in response to barriers and live evidence.

Why it helps: Adaptive teaching avoids fixed ceilings and individualised parallel curricula. Proactive planning and micro-adaptations help more students access the same core learning.

Use it when: when vocabulary, prior knowledge, executive function, sensory access or response evidence shows that the current route is not working for some or many students.

Implementation sequence

Clarify the common learning goal and what will not be lowered.

Anticipate barriers and prepare models, visuals, prompts, vocabulary and alternative explanations.

Check everyone frequently and look for patterns rather than isolated slips.

Make the smallest useful adaptation: rephrase, model, scaffold, regroup, slow down or add guided practice.

Fade support and recheck progress towards the shared standard.

DODON'T
Teach to the top while planning a way in. Use flexible groupings tied to the current barrier, not a permanent label. Prompt thinking before directly supplying answers. Create easier endpoints that cap attainment. Use the same scaffold for all students regardless of evidence. Let support become permanent or dependency-producing.

Check for impact

More students reach the common success criteria with decreasing support.

Groupings and scaffolds change as the evidence changes.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Adaptive Teaching; BSCS Policy: universal provision and anticipated barriers.
