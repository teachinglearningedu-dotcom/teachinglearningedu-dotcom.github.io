Checks for listening

Brief, planned prompts that require students to reproduce, reframe or act on what has just been said, showing whether attention became usable information.

What it is: Brief, planned prompts that require students to reproduce, reframe or act on what has just been said, showing whether attention became usable information.

Why it helps: Quiet appearance is a poor proxy for listening. A low-stakes check makes attention accountable and allows the teacher to repair missed instructions or key explanation points immediately.

Use it when: after instructions, definitions, a peer contribution or a key sentence in an explanation.

Implementation sequence

Plan the sentence, instruction or relationship that students must retain.

Warn the class that listening will be checked and secure full attention.

Use prompts such as 'Where will we answer?', 'What is step one?' or 'What did X say?'.

Use Explain-Frame-Reframe with cold call or choral response.

Correct least-intrusively, insist on 100% and repeat the instruction only when necessary.

DODON'T
Use narrated countdowns and observable attention directions. Pause then name when cold calling the check. Check both the forward and reverse relationship in important statements. Assume silence means the instruction was processed. Ask only 'Are we clear?' or 'Does everyone understand?'. Repeat every instruction automatically, training students not to listen first time.

Check for impact

Students can accurately state or enact the instruction without peer copying.

Lost time from repeated explanations and instructions reduces.

Sources informing this profile: Tips for Teachers: Checks for Listening; BSCS Policy: Active Listening and Signal for Attention.
