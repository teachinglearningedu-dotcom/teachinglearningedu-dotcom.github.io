Participation ratio

Participation ratio is the proportion of pupils actively engaged in the learning task at a given moment. Two variables matter: how many pupils are participating and for how long. Independent practice is a common point for the ratio to fall because pupils have more opportunity for a “cognitive break”. The aim is therefore sustained thinking, effort and accountability — not pupils merely looking busy.

Before practice begins

  • Secure silence and full attention before revealing or distributing materials that could distract from instructions.
  • Give clear, numbered instructions: what you are looking for, what you are not looking for, and what pupils should do when they finish.
  • Check that pupils listened. Display more complex instructions afterwards so they remain available during practice.
  • Use a clear release cue such as “Ready… [pause]… Go!”.
  • Where helpful, use an Instruction Sandwich or Repeated Instructions Game to strengthen the launch routine.

Protect the first 30 seconds: Golden Silence

The first moments establish the norm. The project summary recommended roughly 30 seconds of “Golden Silence”: thank pupils in advance, stand where the whole room is visible, Be Seen Looking, and correct unobtrusively. A least-invasive sequence can move from look → gesture → proximity → quiet word → anonymous reminder. Reset if the start collapses rather than accepting a weak norm.

Purposeful circulation and active observation

  • After Golden Silence, circulate with a route rather than wandering; eliminate classroom blackspots.
  • Check one thing at a time: effort, timing, a particular error or a presentation feature.
  • Visit strong pupils early as well as pupils likely to struggle; this helps establish the expected standard and prevents teacher attention being monopolised by need.
  • Use Book-to-Board circulation where a whole-class comparison will be useful.
  • Carry worked answers and, where useful, notes of anticipated misconceptions.
  • Take physical notes of patterns so you can decide whether to intervene individually or stop the class.
  • Step back from individual desks frequently to preserve whole-class visibility.

Individual intervention sequence

  • 1. Give specific praise for what is already successful.
  • 2. Give one precise next step or model.
  • 3. Check the pupil listened.
  • 4. Check they understand what to do next.
  • 5. Return later and re-check rather than assuming the intervention worked.

A useful MWB intervention from the project notes is: “Show me how you got that” → “Watch me model” → pupil retries → teacher returns later.

Whole-class interruption or leave them working?

Do not interrupt productive practice simply because the teacher has noticed something interesting. Stop the class when a common issue means continued practice would embed error, or when a brief Book-to-Board Quick Check will improve the work of many pupils. Otherwise preserve the uninterrupted practice time.

30:30:30 and 4–2 cycles

  • 30:30:30: roughly 30 seconds silence to establish the start, 30 seconds circulating, 30 seconds standing back and observing the whole class before deciding the next move.
  • 4–2 cycles: four minutes of silent independent practice followed by two minutes of paired discussion, then a return to independent work. The talk is a deliberate rehearsal / checking break, not permission for the whole practice phase to become collaborative.

Diagnostic review during practice

Short checks can be built into longer practice: a Critical Question, Book-to-Board Quick Check, or a prompt such as “Who got 8/10?” can identify whether a common error needs class-wide attention. The teacher’s response should be proportionate to the pattern, not automatic.

Explicit link to the BSCS model

  • Secure learning through practice: Directly strengthens purposeful independent practice, fluency and active observation.
  • Climate and culture: Golden Silence, Be Seen Looking and precise release routines create the conditions for sustained work.
  • Drive thinking for all: Participation ratio is the explicit success measure: number of pupils thinking × duration of thinking.
  • Check and adapt: Active observation, Critical Questions and class-wide interruptions are used to adapt practice from evidence.
