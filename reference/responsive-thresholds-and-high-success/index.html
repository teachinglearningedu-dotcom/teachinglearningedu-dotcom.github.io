Responsive thresholds and high success

Using approximate success bands to choose a proportionate response while keeping practice in a productive zone, commonly around 80% success.

What it is: Using approximate success bands to choose a proportionate response while keeping practice in a productive zone, commonly around 80% success.

Why it helps: High success supports motivation and reduces error practice, but perfection can indicate insufficient challenge. Thresholds turn response data into a practical decision rule rather than a vague impression.

Use it when: after a whole-class check, guided practice or a Do Now item when the teacher must decide whether to move on, use peers or reteach.

Implementation sequence

At roughly 80% or more correct, state or model the answer efficiently, move on and follow up the small group later.

At roughly 40-80% correct, use structured peer explanation or comparison, then recheck with a related but different question.

Below roughly 40% correct, provide a concise teacher explanation or model because there is too little secure knowledge in the room for peer teaching.

If performance remains weak after a response, track the gap and plan further teaching rather than endlessly extending the current activity.

Increase challenge gradually when success is consistently above about 90%.

DODON'T
Treat the percentages as decision aids rather than rigid laws. Prepare follow-up questions and example-problem pairs in advance. Keep correction immediate and supportive. Probe or explain at length when nearly everyone is already correct. Use peer discussion when the room lacks sufficient correct knowledge. Move on below the threshold without a clear repair and recheck.

Check for impact

The response is proportionate to the actual class pattern.

A fresh check, not confidence or noise level, confirms improvement.

Sources informing this profile: Tips for Teachers: Responsive Teaching and Turn and Talk; DistilED: Obtain a High Success Rate.
