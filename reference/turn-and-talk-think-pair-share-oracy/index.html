Turn and Talk / Think-Pair-Share / Oracy

Structured talk that begins with individual thinking, uses a focused paired exchange and ends with accountable sharing, writing or rechecking.

What it is: Structured talk that begins with individual thinking, uses a focused paired exchange and ends with accountable sharing, writing or rechecking.

Why it helps: Exploratory talk helps students retrieve, organise, test and refine ideas. Rehearsal lowers the risk of public response, while explicit listening routines prevent domination and off-task talk.

Use it when: questions requiring explanation, comparison, justification or evaluation, especially when roughly 40-80% of the class has enough knowledge to help one another.

Implementation sequence

Give silent thinking or writing time before talk.

State the question, desired product, time limit and who speaks first.

Use prompts such as disagree and convince, add evidence, or explain why another answer differs.

Circulate without hovering; note strong ideas and misconceptions.

End on the crest of the wave, then cold call, compare responses or require summary writing.

Recheck with a related but different question when talk was used responsively.

DODON'T
Make the pair the unit of accountability and praise effective collaboration. Test listening by asking students to report or evaluate their partner's idea. Use stable pairs and rehearsed switch/return cues. Use talk when fewer than about 40% have enough secure knowledge to contribute. Let discussion continue until energy and focus fade. Accept one partner dominating while the other remains unprepared.

Check for impact

Both partners can explain the shared conclusion and their reasoning.

A fresh check improves after discussion rather than merely sounding fluent.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Oracy for Learning and Think-Pair-Share; Tips for Teachers: Turn and Talk.
