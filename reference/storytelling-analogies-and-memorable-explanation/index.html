Storytelling, analogies and memorable explanation

Using a concise narrative, analogy or metaphor to organise causal relationships and make an abstract idea easier to understand and remember.

What it is: Using a concise narrative, analogy or metaphor to organise causal relationships and make an abstract idea easier to understand and remember.

Why it helps: A coherent narrative can build schema and direct attention through causality, conflict, complication and character. Its value depends on repeated connection to the concept rather than entertainment alone.

Use it when: content with causal sequences, human decisions, competing mechanisms or an abstract relationship that benefits from a familiar structure.

Implementation sequence

Work backwards from the one message or relationship students must retain.

Choose a narrative structure and a single clear thread.

Script concrete details, vocabulary and cause-and-effect links.

Use delivery, gesture and pauses to signal the key moments.

Return explicitly to the academic concept and test the mapping.

State where the analogy stops working to prevent misconception.

DODON'T
Keep the story concise and purposeful. Repeat the key concept at several points. Ask students to explain the connection, not merely retell the story. Include entertaining detail unrelated to the learning goal. Use an analogy with important misleading features left unqualified. Let students remember the story while missing the disciplinary idea.

Check for impact

Students use the narrative to explain the causal or conceptual structure.

They can identify both the useful mapping and its limit.
