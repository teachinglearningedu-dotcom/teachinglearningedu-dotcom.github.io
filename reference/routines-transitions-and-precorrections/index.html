Routines, transitions and precorrections

Teaching repeated classroom processes explicitly and priming students immediately before predictable difficulty points.

What it is: Teaching repeated classroom processes explicitly and priming students immediately before predictable difficulty points.

Why it helps: Routines reduce decision load and maximise learning time. Precorrections make the desired behaviour salient before the moment, preventing rather than merely reacting to predictable errors.

Use it when: movement, equipment, pair talk, practical work, book distribution, transitions and any routine with repeated friction.

Implementation sequence

Script the routine as a short sequence of observable actions.

Explain the purpose, model it and check behavioural understanding.

Rehearse in chunks, then practise the complete sequence with a visible time limit.

Before a high-risk moment, prime the likely error and the desired alternative.

Scan, narrate success and reset only the part that needs more practice.

DODON'T
Use concise cues that become shared classroom language. Assign roles for complex transitions. Review routines after holidays or when membership changes. Assume students know the routine because it was explained once. Correct after the transition without teaching what should replace the error. Overload the routine with too many verbal steps.

Check for impact

Transitions become faster and require fewer teacher prompts.

The predictable error decreases after a specific precorrection.

Sources informing this profile: DistilED: Classroom Transitions and Precorrections; StepFinder: Establish Routines.
