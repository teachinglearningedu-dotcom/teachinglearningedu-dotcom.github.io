Pupil need

Pupils struggle when working independently

Pupils seem to follow the model but make many errors, stall or ask for help as soon as they work alone.

Where to start

Independence is built in stages. Show a complete model, practise together, check readiness, then remove support deliberately.

Strategies to consider

Choose one. Start with a core strategy unless another fits your class better.

  1. Worked examples Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use a worked example when students need to see the full structure of a successful response before attempting a complex task. Make the important decisions and criteria visible.

    Example goal: I will show and explain a complete worked example before students practise, so that students understand the full structure and make fewer errors in independent work.

  2. I Do We Do You Do Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use I Do, We Do, You Do to move students from observing a clear model to accurate independence. The phases may repeat; readiness determines the handover.

    Example goal: I will move from a clear model to guided practice to independence, checking readiness before each handover, so that students reach accurate independent work.

  3. Guided practice Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use guided practice when students have seen an explanation but are not yet ready to perform independently. Keep the demand closely aligned with the model and inspect each important decision.

    Example goal: I will use short guided practice with checks of every student before independent work, so that students only move on when they can perform accurately.

  4. Completion problems Strong evidence base

    Use completion problems to bridge a full worked example and an independent solution. Keep part of the successful structure visible while students supply the missing decisions.

    Example goal: I will use partly completed examples that students finish, so that students bridge from a worked example to independent problems with fewer errors.

  5. Fading scaffolds Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use fading to move students beyond dependence on prompts, models or frames. Decide which support to remove from evidence of what students can now do.

    Example goal: I will plan how to remove prompts and frames step by step based on student success, so that students can complete the task independently without relying on support.

  6. High success rates Research-informed

    Use success-rate evidence to judge whether practice is productive and students are ready for greater independence. Interpret accuracy alongside reasoning, task demand and the distribution of errors.

    Example goal: I will check success rates before releasing students to independent practice, so that students practise at high accuracy and build secure knowledge.

  7. Independent practice Research-informedCore strategy

    Use independent practice to build accurate, sustained performance after explanation and guided rehearsal. Plan the launch, first items and monitoring so students remain thinking throughout.

    Example goal: I will protect a planned block of independent practice with a clear launch and active monitoring, so that students sustain accurate effort without support and I can see what they can do alone.
