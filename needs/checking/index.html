Pupil need

I can't tell who has understood

Answers from a few pupils leave you unsure whether the class is ready to move on, and errors surface later in independent work.

Where to start

Collect evidence from every pupil at the points where it would change your next decision, then act on what you see.

Strategies to consider

Choose one. Start with a core strategy unless another fits your class better.

  1. Mini-whiteboards Research-informedCore strategy

    Make every student’s answer visible, identify gaps and decide what to teach next. Useful before independent practice or whenever answers from a few students leave you unsure about everyone else.

    Example goal: I will use mini-whiteboards with a clear routine at key decision points, so that I see every student's answer and adapt teaching to address gaps.

  2. Hinge questions Research-informedCore strategy

    Use a hinge question at a decision point where students’ answers should change what happens next. Plan the question and the teaching response together.

    Example goal: I will plan a hinge question at a key point in the lesson, so that I decide whether to move on or reteach based on evidence from every student.

  3. Checking for understanding during explanations Research-informedCore strategy

    Use checks during and between explanation chunks to find out what students can recall, distinguish or apply. Gather evidence that informs a specific teaching decision.

    Example goal: I will check understanding of every student between explanation chunks, so that misconceptions are addressed before they build up.

  4. Diagnostic questions Research-informed

    Use diagnostic questions when you need to see which misconception or decision is causing difficulty. A well-designed wrong option tells you something useful about students’ thinking.

    Example goal: I will use one planned multiple-choice diagnostic question per lesson, with wrong options built on common errors, so that students reveal precisely what they misunderstand.

  5. Exit tickets Research-informed

    Use an exit ticket when a brief independent response can inform the next lesson. Choose essential knowledge and a format that can be reviewed quickly.

    Example goal: I will use a short exit ticket on the key learning, so that I identify what to revisit next lesson.

  6. Pause and Fix Research-informedCore strategy

    Use Pause & Fix when a common gap means continuing would reinforce an error or make the next step inaccessible. Stop briefly, repair the precise issue and return to a fresh attempt.

    Example goal: I will stop the class briefly to fix a common misconception when I see it, so that students correct the error before continuing.
