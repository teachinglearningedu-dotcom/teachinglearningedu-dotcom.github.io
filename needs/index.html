What do your pupils need next?

Choose the problem that is closest to what you see in your classroom. Each page suggests a small number of strategies, with the strongest and most relevant first.

Pupils forget what they have been taughtPupils can answer soon after teaching but struggle weeks later, or start new topics without the knowledge they need.Only a few pupils answer questionsThe same hands go up, quieter pupils rarely contribute, and you are not sure the rest of the class is thinking.I can't tell who has understoodAnswers from a few pupils leave you unsure whether the class is ready to move on, and errors surface later in independent work.Pupils struggle when working independentlyPupils seem to follow the model but make many errors, stall or ask for help as soon as they work alone.Pupils don't follow my explanationsExplanations feel clear to you, but pupils lose the thread, cannot repeat the key idea or need it explained again.Pupils keep making the same mistakesThe same errors recur across lessons and assessments, even after you have corrected them.Learning time is lost at the start, transitions or endLessons start slowly, attention drifts during instructions and transitions take longer than they should.Pupils' answers are short or impreciseAnswers are one word, vague or partly correct, and pupils rarely use subject vocabulary accurately.Some pupils can't access the task, or need more challengeThe same task leaves some pupils stuck and others finished early, and adaptations risk lowering the goal.Feedback isn't improving pupils' workYou spend time giving feedback, but the same issues reappear and pupils don't use the comments.
