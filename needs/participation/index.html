Pupil need

Only a few pupils answer questions

The same hands go up, quieter pupils rarely contribute, and you are not sure the rest of the class is thinking.

Where to start

Make thinking everyone's job. Give time to think, then use a routine that expects every pupil to be ready to answer. Make it safe to be wrong.

Strategies to consider

Choose one. Start with a core strategy unless another fits your class better.

  1. Wait time Research-informedCore strategy

    Use wait time to give everyone a chance to retrieve, reason and prepare an answer. Include a pause after a student response before immediately evaluating or asking someone else.

    Example goal: I will pause for at least three to five seconds after asking a question and after an answer, so that more students think and give fuller, more accurate responses.

  2. Cold calling Research-informedCore strategy

    Use cold calling to make classroom questions an invitation for everyone to think. Build a warm, predictable question–pause–name routine and use answers to guide teaching.

    Example goal: I will use question–pause–name cold calling with thinking time, so that all students prepare an answer and I get a representative picture of understanding.

  3. Everyone–Together–Anyone Established practice

    Use Everyone–Together–Anyone to plan how all students think, rehearse and remain accountable. Choose a participation sequence that serves the learning rather than rely on confident volunteers.

    Example goal: I will plan when students respond individually, together or by being selected, so that all students think and participate throughout the lesson.

  4. Mini-whiteboards Research-informedCore strategy

    Make every student’s answer visible, identify gaps and decide what to teach next. Useful before independent practice or whenever answers from a few students leave you unsure about everyone else.

    Example goal: I will use mini-whiteboards with a clear routine at key decision points, so that I see every student's answer and adapt teaching to address gaps.

  5. Everybody Writes / Silent Solo Established practice

    Use Everybody Writes or Silent Solo when students need to form their own response before discussion. Writing gives quieter students a prepared idea and makes initial thinking visible.

    Example goal: I will give students a short silent writing task before discussion, so that every student forms their own response and contributes more thoughtfully.

  6. Making participation safe Established practice

    Use safe participation routines when fear of public error prevents students from contributing. Pair challenge with preparation and respond to uncertainty in predictable, respectful ways.

    Example goal: I will give students thinking or rehearsal time before public answers and respond warmly to errors, so that more students are willing to contribute, including those who are unsure.

  7. Second Chance / No Opt Out Established practice

    Use Second Chance when a student’s first response is “I don’t know” or incomplete. Provide a route to a successful contribution and return to the student.

    Example goal: I will return to a student who says "I don't know" after giving them support, so that every student is expected and helped to contribute successfully.
