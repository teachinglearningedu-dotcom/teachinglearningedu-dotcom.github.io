Pupil need

Some pupils can't access the task, or need more challenge

The same task leaves some pupils stuck and others finished early, and adaptations risk lowering the goal.

Where to start

Keep the goal ambitious for everyone. Check prerequisites, scaffold the specific barrier and plan how support will be removed or challenge increased.

Strategies to consider

Choose one. Start with a core strategy unless another fits your class better.

  1. Prerequisite knowledge checks Research-informed

    Use this before new teaching when students need specific existing knowledge to access the explanation. Check the actual prerequisite rather than asking whether students remember last lesson.

    Example goal: I will check one essential prerequisite with every student before new teaching, so that students start the explanation with the knowledge it depends on.

  2. Scaffolding Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use a scaffold when a specific barrier prevents students from attempting the intended learning successfully. Keep the goal clear and plan how students will work without the support.

    Example goal: I will provide one targeted scaffold for the specific barrier in a task and plan when to remove it, so that students attempt the full learning goal and then succeed without the support.

  3. Adapt and Support Extend and Challenge Research-informedCore strategy

    Use Adapt & Support and Extend & Challenge when responses show different needs. Keep the learning goal ambitious while adjusting the support or depth of the next task.

    Example goal: I will use evidence from checks to give targeted support or challenge while keeping the same learning goal, so that all students make progress from where they are.

  4. Fading scaffolds Strong evidence baseCore strategy

    Use fading to move students beyond dependence on prompts, models or frames. Decide which support to remove from evidence of what students can now do.

    Example goal: I will plan how to remove prompts and frames step by step based on student success, so that students can complete the task independently without relying on support.

  5. ‘What if?’ questions Established practice

    Use What If questions to deepen a secure example by changing one condition. Students should adapt the reasoning they have established rather than begin an unrelated problem.

    Example goal: I will ask "What if...?" questions that change one condition of a secure example, so that students deepen understanding by adapting their reasoning.

  6. Problem-solving Research-informed

    Use problem-solving to help students apply secure knowledge when the route is less familiar. Plan the extra decision demand rather than disguise new teaching inside a complex task.

    Example goal: I will plan problem-solving tasks that apply secure knowledge in less familiar contexts, so that students learn to transfer knowledge and choose an approach independently.
