Wait time

Giving everyone time to think

Use wait time to give everyone a chance to retrieve, reason and prepare an answer. Include a pause after a student response before immediately evaluating or asking someone else.

How to do it

  1. Plan a space-worthy prompt. Use a question that needs retrieval, explanation, justification or evaluation; the required pause should match its complexity.
  2. Set the expectation. Explain that everyone will think before anyone answers and that jotting may be used when the response has several parts.
  3. Wait Time 1: pause after the question. Ask the complete question, then silently count at least three to five seconds—or longer—without rephrasing, filling the silence or signalling a respondent.
  4. Sample an answer. Cold call or use the planned response routine so the protected thinking time belongs to the whole class, not only volunteers.
  5. Wait Time 2: pause after the response. Let the answer land before evaluating it; give peers time to process, then invite elaboration, a build or a challenge.

What it sounds like

“Everyone think silently about which source is more reliable. I will give you time before asking for an answer. Prepare the reason as well as your choice.”

Do

  • Make protected thinking time predictable.
  • Keep every student accountable for the prompt.

Avoid

  • Treating the fastest answer as the best answer.
  • Announcing thinking time and immediately supplying a hint.

Top tips

  • Match the pause to the question. A complex explanation requires more preparation than a brief factual response; no single number fits every prompt.
  • Keep the name until after thinking. Naming a pupil first can signal that others need not prepare.
  • Avoid filling the silence. Rephrasing continuously interrupts the thinking you intended to protect.
  • Use a jot where useful. A short written commitment gives students a stable starting point for a longer response.
  • Preserve the second pause. After an answer, allow others to consider it before asking for agreement, challenge or refinement.

In the classroom

After asking pupils which source better supports a judgement, the teacher gives silent preparation time before naming a respondent. After the answer, another pause allows the class to consider the evidence. A second pupil then compares the reasoning. The question gives more students access than an immediate request for the quickest raised hand.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will pause for at least three to five seconds after asking a question and after an answer, so that more students think and give fuller, more accurate responses.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The teacher pauses before taking answers and after a response.
  • Pupils use the time to think (they do not call out).
  • Answers are more developed and come from more pupils.

Quick check of impact

Record the length and quality of answers before and after introducing deliberate wait time.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
