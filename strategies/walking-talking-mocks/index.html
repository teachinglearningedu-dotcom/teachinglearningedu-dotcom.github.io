Walking-talking mocks

Attempt, diagnose, model and re-check

Use an active walking-talking mock to combine expert modelling with student attempts and diagnosis. Give explanation time where the responses show it is needed.

Evidence: Established practice

This strategy is mainly based on expert practitioner experience and agreed BSCS routines. Research supports the underlying principles indirectly.

Read the evidence summary:

  • Explicit instruction, guided practice and scaffolding
  • Metacognition and self-regulation

How we rate evidence

How to do it

  1. Attempt and commit. Work through one question at a time; students write an answer and confidence rating before any model is shown.
  2. Gather evidence. Use boards for a final answer, first decision or line of working; allocate staff scanning zones where necessary.
  3. Model responsively. Confirm secure work briefly or explain the specific difficulty, making expert decisions visible.
  4. Recheck and stretch. Use a fresh equivalent question after teaching, then a What If variation where the underlying idea is secure.
  5. Create Review Cards. Begin with high-confidence errors, record the mistake and corrected example, and schedule later self-quizzing.

What it sounds like

“Try this question first and rate your confidence. Show the first line of working. We will model the part causing difficulty, then everyone will attempt a related question.”

Do

  • Use responses to decide explanation time.
  • Include a recheck and later retrieval.

Avoid

  • Teacher talk through the whole paper before pupils attempt it.
  • Giving every question identical treatment regardless of need.

Top tips

  • Attempt before the model. Students need a chance to make decisions before seeing the teacher’s solution.
  • Commit confidence before feedback. A score alongside the answer helps identify confidently held errors for later review.
  • Scan in zones. Where several staff are present, assign areas so every response can be seen.
  • Use equivalent follow-ups. A shadow question can check the repaired idea without students simply copying the original.
  • Make the revision usable. Review Cards give students a question they can test themselves on later.

In the classroom

During mock review, pupils attempt a question and rate confidence before staff scan boards in zones. A common first-step error receives a short model and a fresh shadow question. Secure answers receive a purposeful What If variation. Pupils then build Review Cards from selected errors instead of leaving only with an annotated paper.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use walking-talking mocks with attempt, diagnose, model and re-check cycles, so that students improve exam technique and understanding.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Pupils attempt questions before the model.
  • The teacher diagnoses common errors.
  • Pupils re-check or improve their answers.

Quick check of impact

Compare marks on similar questions before and after.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
