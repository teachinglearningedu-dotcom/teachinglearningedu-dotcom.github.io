Cold calling

Involving everyone in questioning

Use cold calling to make classroom questions an invitation for everyone to think. Build a warm, predictable question–pause–name routine and use answers to guide teaching.

How to do it

  1. Establish the rationale and norms. Explain that everyone thinks, the teacher selects and uncertainty is a normal starting point—not a public trap.
  2. Ask the whole class. Pose a clear factual, conceptual or reasoning question before naming a pupil.
  3. Protect wait time. Allow at least several seconds, and longer for complex prompts; scan the room while everyone prepares.
  4. Invite warmly. Name the pupil with open, supportive language and listen without interrupting the first attempt.
  5. Probe or connect. Ask for evidence, a clearer term, a connection or another pupil’s build; require the class to listen.
  6. Support successful re-entry. Use paired or written rehearsal, pre-call, a prompt, a second pupil’s answer and a return to the original pupil when confidence or knowledge is fragile.

What it sounds like

“Without hands up or calling out, everyone think: which evidence supports that claim? [Pause.] Priya, what did you choose and why?”

Do

  • Keep the routine predictable and supportive.
  • Ask follow-up questions about the reasoning.

Avoid

  • Using a question as a sanction for talking.
  • Naming a pupil before others have had time to think.

Top tips

  • Front-load the response mode. Tell students to think without calling out before posing the question.
  • Ask before naming. Everyone prepares, then you select the respondent.
  • Use preparation strategically. Silent thinking, a jot or partner rehearsal helps students develop a response before public contribution.
  • Distribute questions deliberately. Sample across the room, avoiding repeated reliance on confident pupils or random selection without a purpose.
  • Respond to uncertainty constructively. Prompt, provide rehearsal or a Second Chance; do not turn a difficult answer into public punishment.

In the classroom

A teacher asks everyone to consider why a line of code fails, pauses, then names a pupil. An incomplete response receives a targeted clue and preparation time before the teacher returns. The routine keeps the public answer supportive while requiring everyone to think; it is not used to catch out a pupil who was distracted.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use question–pause–name cold calling with thinking time, so that all students prepare an answer and I get a representative picture of understanding.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The question is asked to everyone, followed by a pause, then a name.
  • Cold call is warm and predictable, not used to catch pupils out.
  • A wider range of pupils answer, and answers inform the next step.

Quick check of impact

Track who answers across a few lessons. Note how often you call on non-volunteers.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
