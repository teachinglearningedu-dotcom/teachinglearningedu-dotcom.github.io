Retrieval homework

Revisiting learning beyond the lesson

Use retrieval homework to revisit taught knowledge between lessons. Make the independent attempt possible, provide useful feedback and connect gaps to future teaching.

How to do it

  1. Select the retrieval content. Choose important taught knowledge and procedures, including planned returns to older topics.
  2. Keep the task manageable. State the response, approximate scope and how students should act when stuck.
  3. Protect the attempt. Give instructions to work independently before checking solutions or a support reference.
  4. Build corrective feedback. Provide accurate explanations or worked solutions and a way to record consequential errors.
  5. Connect to the next lesson. Recheck selected items, address common gaps and schedule further retrieval where needed.

What it sounds like

“Try these questions from memory first. Then use the worked solutions to identify errors. Bring one question you need to revisit; we will check the main decisions next lesson.”

Do

  • Set homework through the current BSCS arrangements.
  • Use feedback and later retrieval.

Avoid

  • Untaught independent content as retrieval.
  • Checking completion without attending to the learning.

Top tips

  • Use taught, accessible content. Homework should not rely on students discovering a new method without instruction.
  • Separate attempt and solution. Students need a chance to retrieve before checking worked answers.
  • Mix recent and older learning. A deliberate sequence strengthens return to important knowledge across time.
  • Provide a help route. A specific model, resource or reference supports students when recall fails.
  • Use the result in class. Sample a pivotal item or misconception so homework informs later retrieval and explanation.

In the classroom

A Science homework mixes taught calculations and older retrieval. Pupils attempt before checking worked solutions and record one error to revisit. The next lesson uses a pivotal related question to sample the learning. The homework supports retrieval and feedback rather than becoming independent discovery of a procedure students have never been taught.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will set short retrieval homework on taught content with answers to check against, so that students practise recall between lessons and I can see what needs reteaching.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Homework revisits taught knowledge that pupils can attempt independently.
  • Pupils self-check against answers or a quiz tool.
  • Results feed into a Do Now or reteaching decision.

Quick check of impact

Look at completion rates and whether homework items are recalled better in class.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
