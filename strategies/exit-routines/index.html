Exit routines

Ending lessons calmly and efficiently

Use the agreed BSCS exit sequence to finish learning, restore the room and move students calmly into the corridor. Plan sufficient time for practical equipment and resource return.

How to do it

  1. Stop the academic activity. Secure attention while enough time remains to complete the room’s exit routine.
  2. Explain resource return. Give one clear instruction and check listening before releasing students to act.
  3. Pack in stages. Cue the remaining actions, including resetting equipment and standing ready; keep students who have finished waiting calmly.
  4. Check readiness. Scan the room, correct unfinished actions and move to the threshold before dismissal.
  5. Dismiss deliberately. Release by the agreed row or group sequence, monitor corridor movement and leave the teaching space ready for the next lesson.

What it sounds like

“When I say go, pass the books to the end of your row and then wait. Go. Thank you. Now pack away and stand behind your chair.”

Do

  • Use the agreed sequence and concise instructions.
  • Include room-specific safety and equipment steps.

Avoid

  • Allowing the bell to become the dismissal cue.
  • Giving several competing packing instructions at once.

Top tips

  • End the learning deliberately. Do not let premature packing swallow the final explanation or response.
  • Front-load each action. Tell students what to do before mentioning an object likely to trigger movement.
  • Use one release cue. Students should know when to act and when to wait.
  • Check the room before dismissal. Resource return, workspaces, uniform and equipment need a planned check.
  • Keep sight of the corridor. Dismiss from the threshold in a sequence that allows you to monitor both spaces.

In the classroom

At the end of a practical lesson, the teacher finishes the learning before giving the equipment instructions. Pupils return materials on a clear cue, reset the benches and wait ready. After checking safety and room condition, the teacher dismisses by the agreed sequence from the threshold, maintaining a view of the corridor.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will plan enough time for the agreed exit routine, so that students finish learning, tidy up and leave calmly and on time.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Learning ends on purpose, not because the bell rings.
  • Pupils follow the steps for equipment, standing and leaving.
  • The class leaves calmly and the room is ready for the next group.

Quick check of impact

Note how often lessons overrun or end hurriedly, and the state of the room at the end.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
