Do Now

Preparing students for learning

At BSCS, Do Now provides a short, independent start using spaced retrieval of previously taught, curriculum-critical knowledge. Keep the routine stable so thinking begins without a fresh explanation.

How to do it

  1. Select curriculum-critical knowledge. Use a spaced retrieval plan across recent and older content. Avoid trivia, isolated detail and one-off quiz questions.
  2. Pitch for productive success. Aim for roughly three quarters correct overall: question 1 accessible, questions 2–3 secure for most, question 4 more challenging, plus an optional challenge.
  3. Use clear, single-answer prompts. Prefer discrete facts, features, vocabulary, short procedures or carefully bounded explanations. Do not stack recall, explanation and application into one overloaded prompt.
  4. Make the routine automatic. Display the task in the same place, use a visible timer, keep the response location consistent, and expect quiet independent thinking without notes.
  5. Collect representative evidence. Use mini-whiteboards, Book-to-Board, ABCD cards, call and response or planned indicator pupils rather than volunteers alone.
  6. Respond and reschedule. Correct misconceptions, model briefly where needed, use a related recheck, and return to fragile knowledge in a later Do Now.

What it sounds like

“Complete the four core questions silently, without notes. If you are stuck, move on and return. The final question is an optional challenge.”

Do

  • Protect independent attempts before feedback.
  • Use a timer and proportionate review.

Avoid

  • Retrieving content students have not yet been taught.
  • Turning the Do Now into a long worksheet or lengthy reteaching lesson.

Top tips

  • Use four core questions and a challenge. An accessible first item helps everyone start; later items increase the demand without blocking all progress.
  • Make answers quick to inspect. A bounded response reveals more than an overloaded question with several unrelated demands.
  • Teach a stuck protocol. Students move to another item and return, rather than waiting with no work completed.
  • Make two purposeful laps. First secure participation and the routine; then inspect knowledge and likely errors.
  • Place prerequisites correctly. Check knowledge required for today’s new content at the opening of Explain, rather than replacing spaced retrieval with a starter on today’s topic.

In the classroom

A Languages Do Now retrieves vocabulary from several earlier lessons, with an accessible first item and an optional translation challenge. Students begin independently without notes. The first teacher lap checks the routine and the second samples answers. A shared vocabulary gap gets a brief repair and is scheduled for retrieval again later.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will make the Do Now spaced retrieval of curriculum-critical knowledge that students complete independently in silence, so that students start thinking immediately and strengthen long-term recall.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The Do Now is ready as pupils enter and requires no instruction.
  • Questions retrieve earlier learning, not just last lesson.
  • Pupils work independently and from memory; the teacher scans responses.

Quick check of impact

Track Do Now scores on revisited items over several weeks. Note how quickly all pupils start.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
