Do Now feedback

Checking responses and addressing gaps

Use a proportionate review so Do Now both strengthens recall and informs teaching. Gather the answers first, then decide which items need confirmation, discussion or a brief repair.

How to do it

  1. Collect independent responses. Use boards, a bounded written answer or a deliberate sample before revealing the solution.
  2. Identify the pattern. Distinguish isolated slips, missing knowledge and a shared misconception; note students whose response remains unclear.
  3. Choose the smallest useful response. Confirm secure items briefly, discuss genuine differences or re-model the point causing widespread difficulty.
  4. Recheck the repaired idea. Use a fresh question with comparable demand and enough change to require independent thinking.
  5. Reschedule the knowledge. Record the item for later retrieval and follow up individual gaps during subsequent teaching or practice.

What it sounds like

“Most of us have question one secure, so I will confirm it briefly. Question three shows two different ideas; let us compare those before trying a related question.”

Do

  • Choose the response from the pattern of answers.
  • Keep the review concise and consequential.

Avoid

  • Asking only volunteers for their answers.
  • Automatically reteaching every question in full.

Top tips

  • Do not give every item equal time. A nearly universal correct response rarely needs the same explanation as a common misconception.
  • Keep the solution hidden while scanning. An early answer changes what later responses can tell you.
  • Ask for a fresh response after repair. A slightly changed question shows more than asking students to copy the corrected answer.
  • Use discussion when it has a purpose. Compare different plausible responses, then return to an individual answer so the evidence remains usable.
  • Record fragile knowledge. Revisit it in future retrieval rather than trying to solve every long-term issue in today’s starter.

In the classroom

A class answers most Do Now items accurately, but several confuse evaporation with boiling. The teacher confirms secure items briefly, contrasts the two ideas and asks a fresh question about a puddle drying. That response informs the next move. The distinction returns in a later Do Now instead of consuming the entire starter today.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will check Do Now answers efficiently and address the most important gap, so that students correct errors and I adapt teaching to what they actually know.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Answers are checked quickly (for example, self-marking or mini-whiteboards).
  • The teacher identifies a common error and addresses it briefly.
  • Pupils correct their answers in a different colour or re-attempt.

Quick check of impact

Note which errors recur in later Do Nows and whether the targeted gap closes.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
