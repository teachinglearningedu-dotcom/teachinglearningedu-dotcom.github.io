Explicit vocabulary teaching

Developing keystone vocabulary

Use explicit vocabulary teaching when disciplinary language is essential to understanding and expressing an idea. Students need to hear, say, use and revisit the word in context.

How to do it

  1. Identify the essential term. Choose vocabulary needed to understand the content or produce a precise response.
  2. Say and explain it. Model pronunciation, give a concise definition and connect it to a familiar idea without distorting the meaning.
  3. Show boundaries. Use examples, non-examples and any important contrast with the everyday meaning.
  4. Rehearse active use. Students say, write and apply the term in a sentence, explanation or comparison; prompt precision where necessary.
  5. Return later. Include the term in retrieval and new contexts so students can recognise and use it without the original prompt.

What it sounds like

“In science, work means energy transferred when a force moves an object. Use that definition to explain which of these examples counts as work.”

Do

  • Teach a precise meaning and examples.
  • Build repeated use into explanation and practice.

Avoid

  • Giving a long list of definitions to copy once.
  • Accepting the correct word in an otherwise inaccurate explanation.

Top tips

  • Select keystone vocabulary. Prioritise words that unlock the current concept and recur in later learning.
  • Address familiar meanings. Words such as “work”, “power” and “significant” can have a different disciplinary meaning.
  • Use morphology when useful. A prefix or root can support meaning, but it should not replace the subject definition.
  • Require use in a full idea. Correct pronunciation alone does not show that a student understands the term.
  • Revisit through varied cues. Ask for a definition, an example, a non-example or an explanation using the word.

In the classroom

In Business Studies, pupils learn the disciplinary meaning of profit and contrast it with revenue. They use the terms in a short explanation and classify examples before calculating. Later retrieval asks for the distinction through a new scenario. Correct use depends on meaning rather than recognising a copied definition.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will explicitly teach two or three keystone words per topic, with student-friendly definitions and repeated use, so that students understand and use subject vocabulary accurately in speech and writing.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Key terms are introduced explicitly with a definition, examples and pronunciation.
  • Pupils say, write and use the word, not just hear it.
  • The words are revisited in later lessons and tasks.

Quick check of impact

Look for accurate use of the target words in independent writing and spoken answers.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
