Consolidation

Securing new learning before independent practice

Use consolidation after initial explanation and guided practice to secure the new idea before increasing independence or complexity. Make students reconstruct and explain the learning.

Research-informed

Evidence: Research-informed

This strategy is consistent with research principles, but the evidence for this kind of practice is less direct, more mixed or more dependent on context.

Read the evidence summary:

  • Explicit instruction, guided practice and scaffolding
  • Cognitive science approaches: retrieval, spacing, cognitive load, worked examples and dual coding

How to do it

  1. Select the central learning. Identify the principle or process students should retain from the episode.
  2. Ask for reconstruction. Use a brief explanation, completion task or related attempt that requires students to recall and organise the idea.
  3. Compare thoughtfully. Let students inspect a model or another response and explain meaningful differences.
  4. Repair the insecure component. Re-model a specific gap and give another fresh attempt where necessary.
  5. Connect the next stage. Move into purposeful independent practice or application when sufficiently secure, and plan a later retrieval return.

What it sounds like

“Without looking at the model, explain the first decision and complete a related example. Then compare your method and identify the step you need to practise.”

Do

  • Reconstruct the important idea or process.
  • Use the response to decide readiness.

Avoid

  • A final slide that students passively reread.
  • Assuming immediate success removes the need for later retrieval.

Top tips

  • Keep it focused on the new learning. A broad topic recap may miss the specific decision taught in this episode.
  • Require an active response. Explanation, completion or a short independent attempt shows more than rereading the model.
  • Use the model deliberately. Let students compare initially, then remove it for a fresh attempt.
  • Do not equate consolidation with easy work. Students should think about the key relationship, not merely copy a summary.
  • Connect to later retrieval. Consolidation helps initial security; spaced returns are still needed.

In the classroom

After guided practice, pupils reconstruct the key method and complete a short related task with less visible support. Comparison with the model reveals one insecure decision, which gets a focused repair. A later retrieval return is planned. Consolidation secures the episode without being treated as proof that the learning will remain accessible permanently.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will have students reconstruct and explain the new idea before independent practice, so that students secure the learning and make fewer errors when working alone.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Pupils explain or reconstruct the method without the model.
  • The teacher checks and addresses gaps before moving on.
  • Independent work starts from secure understanding.

Quick check of impact

Compare accuracy in independent practice following consolidation.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
