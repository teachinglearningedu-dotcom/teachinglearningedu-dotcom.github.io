Atomisation

Breaking learning into prerequisite and new knowledge

Use atomisation in lesson preparation to identify the small pieces of knowledge and action a successful performance requires. Separate existing prerequisites from genuinely new learning.

How to do it

  1. Choose the endpoint. Write a representative question, performance or response that embodies the intended learning.
  2. Unpack the actions. Work through it and list the knowledge, interpretations and decisions required at each point.
  3. Separate old and new. Mark components that students should already know and those the lesson must explicitly teach.
  4. Plan the prerequisite checks. Use short direct prompts, repair insecure foundations and recheck before introducing dependent content.
  5. Build the episode. Sequence the new components, a concise model, guided rehearsal and recombination into an independent whole-task attempt.

What it sounds like

“To calculate this mass, students must interpret the formula, find the amount, use the relationship and manage units. Which are prerequisites, and which are new today?”

Do

  • Identify prerequisite and new components explicitly.
  • Plan a direct check for the likely bottleneck.

Avoid

  • Reducing the task to disconnected facts.
  • Assuming a difficult final answer reveals which component failed.

Top tips

  • Start with a real task. A representative example exposes the decisions students actually need to make.
  • Ask what each step depends on. Reading a symbol, selecting a rule and completing an operation may require different knowledge.
  • Check prerequisites separately. A complex final question can hide which small component is missing.
  • Avoid endless fragmentation. Keep the overall concept visible and recombine the pieces after rehearsal.
  • Use the analysis to plan teaching. Atomisation is useful when it changes the explanation, check or practice sequence.

In the classroom

Preparing a gas-volume calculation, the teacher separates interpreting the question, converting units, recalling the relationship and using the amount correctly. A short check reveals that unit conversion is insecure. That prerequisite is repaired before the new application is modelled. Atomisation changes the teaching route rather than merely producing a longer planning list.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will break one complex skill into its component knowledge and check each component, so that students' errors can be traced to a specific missing piece and fixed.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The teacher checks small components separately before combining them.
  • Questions isolate one component at a time.
  • Pupils who struggle with the whole task are given a focused component to fix.

Quick check of impact

Compare success on the full task before and after teaching the components separately.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
