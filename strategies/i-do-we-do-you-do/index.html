I Do We Do You Do

Moving towards independence

Use I Do, We Do, You Do to move students from observing a clear model to accurate independence. The phases may repeat; readiness determines the handover.

Evidence: Strong evidence base

This strategy applies an approach with a strong, consistent research base, such as high-security EEF evidence or well-replicated findings from cognitive science. Research on this exact routine may be more limited, so check how your pupils respond.

How to do it

  1. I Do the model. Explain one coherent example, revealing the method and the reasons for key choices.
  2. We Do a related example. Students supply a decision or step while the teacher guides, checks and corrects.
  3. Check the whole class. Use boards, a brief written response or a practical demonstration rather than assuming readiness from volunteers.
  4. You Do a small attempt. Give a closely matched task that students complete independently, with success criteria and any necessary temporary support.
  5. Increase responsibility. Remove prompts and add variation when performance is sufficiently secure; return to guidance if common errors persist.

What it sounds like

“Watch my example first. You will now try a different example one step at a time. Once those decisions are secure, you will complete the next one independently.”

Do

  • Use evidence to decide the release.
  • Preserve the connection between model and practice.

Avoid

  • Treating phases as fixed time slots.
  • Making You Do substantially harder without teaching the new demand.

Top tips

  • Use different examples. We Do should require the same underlying learning without being a copy of the I Do.
  • Keep the model visible initially. Support comparison without allowing students simply to substitute a superficial detail.
  • Make We Do genuinely participatory. Every student attempts a step; a few pupils supplying answers is not class-wide rehearsal.
  • Release a short first task. Inspect early independent work before students repeat the method across many items.
  • Loop back when needed. Re-model a sticking point rather than persisting with independent work students cannot yet complete.

In the classroom

A teacher models one paragraph, guides a second response using different evidence and collects everyone’s opening claim. Where the claim is secure, pupils attempt another paragraph independently. A common gap in the explanation leads to a short return to modelling. The phases repeat according to readiness rather than a fixed allocation of minutes.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will move from a clear model to guided practice to independence, checking readiness before each handover, so that students reach accurate independent work.

Peer observer look-fors

  • There is a clear I Do (model), We Do (shared) and You Do (independent) sequence.
  • The teacher checks success during We Do before releasing pupils.
  • Pupils attempt You Do with high accuracy.

Quick check of impact

Record the success rate in the first few independent questions.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
