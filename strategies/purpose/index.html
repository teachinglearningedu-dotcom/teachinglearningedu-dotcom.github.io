Purpose

Helping students understand what they are learning and why

Use a clear purpose to help students understand what the lesson is building towards and why the learning matters. Connect activities to the knowledge, performance or later use.

How to do it

  1. Define the destination. State the knowledge or performance students will develop, using language they can understand.
  2. Select a concrete frame. Use an endpoint question, model or meaningful application to show what the learning enables.
  3. Connect the first step. Explain how today’s component contributes to the larger idea.
  4. Keep the purpose visible in practice. Refer to it when choosing examples, checking a decision or explaining an improvement.
  5. Return to the destination. Ask students to use the learning in the original question or a related task so the purpose has a concrete outcome.

What it sounds like

“We are learning how evidence supports a historical judgement. This paragraph gives us a way to compare explanations rather than list events.”

Do

  • Connect the task to a clear learning destination.
  • Use a meaningful subject rationale.

Avoid

  • An activity label presented as the purpose.
  • A lengthy motivational introduction unrelated to the content.

Top tips

  • Name the learning rather than the activity. “Explain the cause” is clearer than “Complete the worksheet.”
  • Keep purpose concrete. A question, performance or problem can make the destination visible.
  • Show a genuine connection. Use the curriculum or subject application rather than an invented claim that every idea is needed in daily life.
  • Revisit at the decision point. Explain how the current step contributes to the endpoint.
  • Preserve curiosity without delaying instruction. A hook is useful when it directs attention towards the learning, not when it becomes a separate entertainment.

In the classroom

A Product Design lesson frames material properties through a concrete selection problem. The teacher explains how each taught property helps justify the choice. Practice returns to that same decision with a different product. The purpose connects activity to disciplinary reasoning rather than an artificial claim that every detail has an immediate everyday use.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will connect each lesson's activities explicitly to what students are building towards, so that students can explain why the learning matters and how it links to earlier work.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The teacher states how today's learning connects to previous and future learning.
  • The purpose is linked to knowledge or performance, not just a task.
  • Pupils can explain why they are learning this when asked.

Quick check of impact

Ask a sample of pupils what they are learning and why. Note whether answers refer to knowledge, not just activities.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
