High success rates

Deciding when students are ready for independence

Use success-rate evidence to judge whether practice is productive and students are ready for greater independence. Interpret accuracy alongside reasoning, task demand and the distribution of errors.

  • Mastery learning

How we rate evidence

How to do it

  1. Sequence for early success. Teach one manageable component at a time and anticipate misconceptions before complexity is added.
  2. Model and guide. Make an accurate first example visible, then co-construct and rehearse several short examples with high participation.
  3. Check accuracy and reasoning. Use all-pupil responses, short quizzes or sampled work to estimate the class success rate and the pattern of errors.
  4. Adjust support or challenge. Below the productive range, re-model, shrink the step or restore a scaffold. When accuracy is consistently above about 90%, fade support or add depth, variation or transfer.
  5. Reinforce and revisit. Use immediate correction, later retrieval and spaced practice so accurate responses become fluent and durable.

What it sounds like

“The answers are mostly accurate. Before you work independently, explain the first decision on a new example. I will check the remaining gaps while you practise.”

Do

  • Use accuracy, reasoning and task structure together.
  • Adjust support before repeated failure.

Avoid

  • Mechanical release at a fixed percentage.
  • Making every practice task trivial to achieve high scores.

Top tips

  • Treat percentages as signals. Around 80% can be a useful practical reference in some tasks, not a universal rule or guarantee of readiness.
  • Inspect what the errors concern. A small minority missing a crucial prerequisite still needs follow-up.
  • Distinguish accuracy from copying. Success with an identical example or visible answer can exaggerate competence.
  • Increase challenge when secure. Fade support, change a condition or require explanation instead of assigning more of the same.
  • Return to the learning. Initial success is a starting point for fluency and later retrieval, not proof of permanent mastery.

In the classroom

Most guided answers are correct, but several students have copied a visible method without explaining the first decision. The teacher uses a distinct example and collects reasoning before release. Secure pupils receive a useful variation while the remaining group gets targeted guidance. Readiness is judged from the demand and pattern, not the percentage alone.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will check success rates before releasing students to independent practice, so that students practise at high accuracy and build secure knowledge.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The teacher checks accuracy before moving on.
  • Most pupils show success on checks before practice.
  • The teacher adjusts if success is low.

Quick check of impact

Compare success rates at release with performance in independent work.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
