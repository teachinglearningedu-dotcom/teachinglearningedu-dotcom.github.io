Adapt and Support Extend and Challenge

Responding to different needs

Use Adapt & Support and Extend & Challenge when responses show different needs. Keep the learning goal ambitious while adjusting the support or depth of the next task.

How to do it

  1. Read the pattern. Identify which students need a prompt, a re-model or more guided practice and which are ready for deeper application.
  2. Locate the barrier. Check whether the issue concerns knowledge, vocabulary, task structure or a specific decision.
  3. Select the smallest support. Give a useful cue, model, frame or short teacher group while retaining the curricular goal.
  4. Extend the secure learning. Use explanation, variation or a less familiar case that requires additional thinking.
  5. Hand back responsibility. Remove support as competence becomes visible and use independent responses to decide the next adjustment.

What it sounds like

“If you need the first-step prompt, use it for this example. If your method is secure, explain how the answer changes when this condition changes.”

Do

  • Adjust support to the observed barrier.
  • Provide meaningful challenge for secure students.

Avoid

  • Permanent task assignment by label or prior attainment.
  • Giving different groups unrelated learning goals.

Top tips

  • Diagnose before grouping. The same incorrect answer can come from different barriers; a quick probe may change the support needed.
  • Use flexible groups. Group briefly by the current learning need, then release students when they can proceed independently.
  • Extend through depth. Ask for justification, a boundary case or transfer rather than simply more identical questions.
  • Preserve a common thread. Students should remain working on the intended knowledge even when support differs.
  • Return to independent evidence. A successful response beside the teacher may depend on prompts; check what the student can do alone.

In the classroom

In Science, some pupils need a prompt to select the relationship while others can justify it independently. A short teacher group rehearses the first decision; secure pupils explore how a changed condition affects the result. All work remains connected to the same concept, and the temporary support is removed when students can proceed alone.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use evidence from checks to give targeted support or challenge while keeping the same learning goal, so that all students make progress from where they are.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Decisions are based on evidence from the class.
  • Support or challenge is targeted and the goal is unchanged.
  • Pupils work at an appropriate level of challenge.

Quick check of impact

Review work samples from supported and extended pupils for progress towards the same goal.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
