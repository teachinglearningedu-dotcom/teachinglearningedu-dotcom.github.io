The visualiser

Live modelling step by step

Use the visualiser to show how a response is constructed, including decisions, annotations and revisions. Students see the sequence that a finished example can hide.

How to do it

  1. Prepare the surface. Position the visualiser, test the view and lay out the page so key work remains visible without constant movement.
  2. State the goal. Explain what students should attend to and connect the model to the intended learning.
  3. Construct the response. Write, draw or demonstrate one coherent section, narrating the consequential choices rather than every physical action.
  4. Pause deliberately. Check listening, let students read an annotation or ask them to explain a decision before adding complexity.
  5. Move into practice. Give a distinct but related example, keep the model available initially and gather everyone’s first attempt before releasing longer work.

What it sounds like

“Watch this first decision without copying. I am choosing this evidence because it supports the claim. Now pause and identify the feature that made it useful.”

Do

  • Make decisions and revisions visible.
  • Follow the model with a different guided example.

Avoid

  • Projecting an unreadable polished answer.
  • Talking continuously while students try to copy every line.

Top tips

  • Check the back row view. Focus, contrast, orientation and text size matter before the modelling starts.
  • Rehearse the model. Decide where the difficult choice, likely error and pause will occur; do not improvise the whole process.
  • Separate watching from copying. When the decision is novel, protect attention to the model and provide recording time afterwards.
  • Control where students look. Point to the relevant line or feature, and step away from the display when you want attention on you.
  • Preserve a useful model. Keep it available during early practice, then remove it when students are ready to attempt independently.

In the classroom

A Product Design teacher sketches a joint under the visualiser, narrating why each feature is positioned. Students watch the unfamiliar construction first, then annotate an available model. Their guided task uses a different set of dimensions. The visualiser makes the drawing sequence and decisions visible without turning the lesson into simultaneous copying.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will model answers live under the visualiser, narrating each decision, so that students see how a strong response is built and can construct their own.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The teacher writes or works live, not just showing a finished answer.
  • Decisions, annotations and corrections are explained aloud.
  • Pupils' work reflects the modelled structure and decisions.

Quick check of impact

Compare pupils' independent responses with the model. Note whether the targeted features appear.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
