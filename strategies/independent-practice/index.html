Independent practice

Helping students begin and sustain effort

Use independent practice to build accurate, sustained performance after explanation and guided rehearsal. Plan the launch, first items and monitoring so students remain thinking throughout.

Evidence: Research-informed

This strategy is consistent with research principles, but the evidence for this kind of practice is less direct, more mixed or more dependent on context.

Read the evidence summary:

  • Explicit instruction, guided practice and scaffolding
  • Metacognition and self-regulation

How we rate evidence

How to do it

  1. Prepare for success. Use evidence from guided practice to confirm that most pupils can complete the process accurately and identify who still needs a scaffold or teacher check.
  2. Rehearse before release. Correct common misconceptions, make the success criteria visible and clarify what pupils should do if they become stuck.
  3. Mirror before you leap. Begin with the same method, representation and type of material used in guided practice. Do not hide new knowledge inside an 'independent' task.
  4. Monitor in motion. Circulate from the first item, sample the whole room and intervene before an error is repeated across the set.
  5. Build fluency through repetition. Secure accuracy first, then reduce hesitation, increase volume or add carefully selected variation.
  6. Connect to future retrieval. Return to the learning after a delay and mix it with related content so performance becomes durable and flexible.

What it sounds like

“Begin with the first two items independently. If stuck, identify the step that is unclear and use the model. When finished, explain why your method works.”

Do

  • Check readiness before release.
  • Use a route that includes the whole room.

Avoid

  • Hiding new learning inside the independent task.
  • Equating a quiet classroom with sustained thinking.

Top tips

  • Protect the first moments. A brief Golden Silence establishes the expectation before circulation begins.
  • Teach what to do when stuck. Students can check the model, identify the uncertain step or move to a permitted item; repeated hand-up waiting reduces participation.
  • Inspect the first attempt. Early diagnosis prevents a whole page of repeated errors.
  • Provide a meaningful finish task. Deepen the same learning through explanation or variation rather than unrelated extra work.
  • Preserve productive time. Interrupt only when a common issue makes the class pause worthwhile.

In the classroom

A teacher begins independent practice with two familiar items, clear success criteria and a taught stuck protocol. A short observation establishes the working norm before circulation. Early answers reveal one shared error, so the class receives a bounded repair. Pupils then return to productive practice with a meaningful explanation task for early finishers.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will protect a planned block of independent practice with a clear launch and active monitoring, so that students sustain accurate effort without support and I can see what they can do alone.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Independent practice is protected and long enough to be meaningful.
  • Pupils start quickly and work independently.
  • The teacher monitors and responds to errors without taking over.

Quick check of impact

Record the amount completed and the accuracy across several lessons.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
