Exit tickets

Identifying what needs revisiting

Use an exit ticket when a brief independent response can inform the next lesson. Choose essential knowledge and a format that can be reviewed quickly.

How to do it

  1. Choose the purpose. Identify the essential idea or prerequisite you need to inspect before the next lesson.
  2. Write a short prompt. Use a bounded explanation, diagnostic choice, diagram or small performance that reveals the target knowledge.
  3. Allow an independent response. Reserve enough time and keep the solution hidden until students have committed.
  4. Divide and inspect. Sort by quality of understanding, then look closely at the main error patterns and selected unclear answers.
  5. Decide the next lesson response. Plan a fresh retrieval or diagnostic prompt and the explanation, rehearsal or support the evidence warrants.

What it sounds like

“Before you leave, answer this alone: which example shows diffusion, and which feature tells you? Include one sentence explaining your choice.”

Do

  • Target the learning that matters for the next lesson.
  • Use the responses to plan teaching.

Avoid

  • Collecting tickets without reviewing them.
  • A long end-of-lesson assessment with no time to complete it.

Top tips

  • Keep the ticket narrow. One useful conceptual distinction may reveal more than five broad questions.
  • Include reasoning where necessary. A letter choice alone can hide why the student selected it.
  • Protect independence. A ticket copied after class discussion gives less information about the individual’s understanding.
  • Sort before annotating. Group responses by secure, partly secure and insecure, then inspect the causes of difficulty.
  • Prepare the next move. Decide whether tomorrow needs retrieval, a brief clarification, guided practice or a fuller reteach.

In the classroom

A Computing exit ticket asks pupils to predict the output of a short, taught code structure and explain one decision. The teacher sorts the answers and finds a common misunderstanding. The next lesson begins with a related check and a short repair, rather than starting new content over an unresolved prerequisite.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use a short exit ticket on the key learning, so that I identify what to revisit next lesson.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The exit ticket targets essential knowledge and takes only a few minutes.
  • Pupils complete it independently.
  • The results inform the next lesson's plan.

Quick check of impact

Check whether the next lesson addresses the gaps the exit ticket revealed.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
