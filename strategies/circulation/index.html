Circulation

Sampling independent work and responding

Use circulation to read learning across the room, identify the exact error and choose a response. Move with a route and a diagnostic focus rather than following raised hands alone.

How to do it

  1. Plan the route. Identify the success criterion, likely errors and pupils you must see, with a path that covers every area.
  2. Observe first attempts. After securing the launch, inspect early work before students repeat the process many times.
  3. Diagnose the response. Read the relevant section and ask a brief question where the reasoning is unclear.
  4. Give a bounded next action. Use a prompt, clue or direct explanation according to need, then require the pupil to do the thinking.
  5. Revisit and decide. Check the change; preserve productive work where gaps are isolated, or pause the class when the pattern justifies reteaching.

What it sounds like

“I am checking the reason you chose this method. Show me that decision. Fix this line, then try the next item; I will return shortly.”

Do

  • Use scan, read and compare with the criterion.
  • Return after a targeted intervention.

Avoid

  • Wandering without a focus.
  • Explaining at length beside one pupil while losing the rest of the room.

Top tips

  • Inspect one feature at a time. Look for the first decision, selected evidence or a specific presentation feature so the scan stays purposeful.
  • Include likely invisible pupils. Deliberately visit students whose work or thinking you do not normally hear.
  • Visit strong work early too. It helps establish the expected standard and can supply an exemplar for later discussion.
  • Keep the whole room visible. Step back between individual interactions; avoid becoming absorbed at one desk.
  • Record patterns briefly. A quick note or seating-plan mark helps distinguish an individual gap from a class-wide issue.

In the classroom

During essay practice, the teacher’s route checks the claim–evidence link in selected first paragraphs across the room. Quick notes distinguish missing evidence from weak explanation. Some pupils receive an individual prompt; a shared pattern becomes a brief class model. The teacher returns to inspect the changed sentence before continuing the route.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will circulate with a planned route and focus during independent work, so that I spot common errors quickly and respond to them.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Circulation follows a deliberate route, not only raised hands.
  • The teacher looks for specific errors.
  • Feedback is brief, or a common issue is addressed with the whole class.

Quick check of impact

Note how quickly common errors are identified and addressed.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
