Golden Silence and active observation

Launching independent practice

Use Golden Silence and active observation to protect the launch of independent practice. Establish the working norm, then alternate purposeful circulation with a view of the whole class.

How to do it

  1. Prepare the launch. Give clear instructions, a stuck protocol and a useful finish task, then check listening before release.
  2. Protect Golden Silence. Use a brief period of visible whole-class observation to establish independent effort.
  3. Circulate purposefully. Inspect a specific feature of early work along a planned route and give short targeted feedback.
  4. Step back and decide. Regain a view of the room, identify participation gaps and distinguish individual need from a common error.
  5. Continue or reset. Preserve productive practice, use a brief class repair when justified, or a planned discussion break followed by renewed independent work.

What it sounds like

“Begin independently. I will watch the first thirty seconds, then check first attempts. If you need help, identify the step that is unclear while continuing what you can.”

Do

  • Secure the start before extended individual help.
  • Step back regularly to observe the whole room.

Avoid

  • Circulating into a long conversation immediately.
  • Treating silent appearance as evidence that everyone is thinking.

Top tips

  • Protect roughly the first thirty seconds. Stand where you can see the room, recognise the expected start and correct unobtrusively.
  • Use 30:30:30 flexibly. A brief launch, circulation period and step-back observation can stop individual support absorbing all attention.
  • Eliminate blackspots. A planned route should reach every part of the room, not just raised hands.
  • Give one precise intervention. Prompt the next step, check it was heard and return after the student works.
  • Use 4–2 cycles selectively. A short independent burst followed by paired checking can sustain thinking when it serves the task; it is not a compulsory timer routine.

In the classroom

After checking instructions, the teacher protects a brief silent launch from a position with a whole-room view. Circulation then checks first attempts along a route, followed by a step back to inspect participation. A targeted prompt is revisited later. The teacher avoids spending the opening minutes absorbed at one desk.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will launch independent practice with a short silent start while I observe the room, so that students begin work independently and I can spot difficulties early.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Practice begins with an explicit silent working period.
  • The teacher scans and circulates with purpose.
  • Pupils start immediately and work independently.

Quick check of impact

Note how quickly all pupils start and how many need help in the first few minutes.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
