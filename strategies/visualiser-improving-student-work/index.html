The visualiser

Discussing and improving student work

Use the visualiser to make student work a shared learning resource. Select a response because it illustrates a useful success, misconception or improvement decision.

How to do it

  1. Select the response. Choose a useful exemplar or near-miss and identify the learning feature it will reveal.
  2. Prepare the display. Check readability, crop or mask irrelevant details and frame the sharing respectfully.
  3. Ask students to inspect. Give silent time and a specific question about the criterion before taking contributions.
  4. Model improvement. Annotate or edit the relevant section live, explaining the effect of each important change.
  5. Require transfer. Students apply the same criterion to their own work or a fresh example, then you sample the resulting improvement.

What it sounds like

“This paragraph gives a clear claim. Let us find where the explanation stops short. We will improve that link, then you will inspect the same feature in your own paragraph.”

Do

  • Make the criterion and revision visible.
  • Use contrasting responses to clarify quality.

Avoid

  • Displaying work solely to celebrate presentation.
  • Critiquing a named pupil rather than the response.

Top tips

  • Select with a purpose. Decide which feature the class should notice before projecting the work.
  • Establish a respectful culture. Sharing should feel routine; anonymise where useful and avoid public embarrassment.
  • Keep the discussion narrow. One paragraph, diagram or process step often produces a clearer learning focus than the whole page.
  • Show the edit live. Explain why the change improves the response, retaining successful features.
  • Transfer to students’ own work. Everyone should make an equivalent improvement rather than merely watch the projected edit.

In the classroom

A teacher projects an anonymised paragraph that has a strong claim but a weak explanation. Pupils inspect the criterion, then watch a live edit and explain why it improves the link. Everyone makes an equivalent improvement in their own response. The projected work creates shared learning rather than a public judgement of its author.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use the visualiser to discuss and improve selected student work, so that students see what quality looks like and improve their own work.

Peer observer look-fors

  • The work is chosen to show a useful success or improvement.
  • The teacher and pupils discuss and annotate it.
  • Pupils apply the improvements to their own work.

Quick check of impact

Look for the discussed features in pupils' subsequent work.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
