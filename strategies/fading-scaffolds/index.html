Fading scaffolds

Removing support as students become secure

Use fading to move students beyond dependence on prompts, models or frames. Decide which support to remove from evidence of what students can now do.

How to do it

  1. Identify the remaining dependency. Observe which part of the task still requires the model, cue or teacher prompt.
  2. Select the next reduction. Remove one support that successful performance suggests is no longer needed.
  3. Give a comparable attempt. Hold the task demand steady so you can interpret the effect of the reduction.
  4. Respond to the attempt. Keep, reduce or briefly restore a specific cue according to accuracy and reasoning.
  5. Hand over the process. Students plan and complete the task independently, then revisit it later and in a varied context.

What it sounds like

“You no longer need the sentence opening. Keep the evidence checklist for this attempt; next time we will try without that too.”

Do

  • Make the withdrawal staged and purposeful.
  • Check the decisions students make without prompts.

Avoid

  • Keeping a frame permanently for every task.
  • Removing all support on the same date for every student.

Top tips

  • Separate support from difficulty. Keep content familiar while removing a cue, then introduce variation once independence is secure.
  • Fade a specific feature. Remove the sentence opening, checklist item or worked step that the student no longer needs.
  • Watch for hidden dependence. Successful copying from a visible model does not establish that the student can choose the approach alone.
  • Restore the minimum cue. If success drops, reinstate the smallest support that repairs the barrier.
  • Include later independence. Return after a delay so the task is not supported by immediate memory of the model.

In the classroom

For an extended response, the teacher first removes the sentence starters but keeps the evidence checklist. After pupils can explain the links independently, the checklist is reduced too. When one student loses the structure, only the necessary cue returns. Support changes with the observed barrier rather than disappearing for the whole class at once.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will plan how to remove prompts and frames step by step based on student success, so that students can complete the task independently without relying on support.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Support is reduced in planned stages.
  • The teacher uses evidence of success to decide when to fade.
  • Pupils complete later tasks with less or no support.

Quick check of impact

Record how many pupils complete the final task without the scaffold.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
