Review Cards

Turning errors into future retrieval

Use Review Cards to turn corrected errors into a personal retrieval resource. Separate the question from the solution so students can attempt it again without seeing the answer.

How to do it

  1. Select the error. Choose a consequential gap from a quiz, assessment or mock, prioritising a confidently held mistake where useful.
  2. Write the front. Copy a complete question or prompt that can be answered independently.
  3. Build the back. Record the topic, original mistake, corrected method and annotations that explain the crucial decision.
  4. Add support. Include a specific reference for help or further practice, checking that the solution is accurate.
  5. Use the deck. Attempt the front before checking the back; revisit incorrect cards sooner and return to secure cards after a longer interval.

What it sounds like

“Put the question on the front. On the back, record the topic, your mistake, a corrected worked example and the note that would help you avoid it next time.”

Do

  • Keep the question and solution on opposite sides.
  • Revisit the cards through independent attempts.

Avoid

  • A card containing only a final answer.
  • Making cards that students never use for retrieval.

Top tips

  • Begin with a high-confidence error. It identifies an important mismatch between certainty and correctness.
  • Annotate for the future self. Include why the decisive step works and what caused the original mistake.
  • Add a help reference. Link to an appropriate worked example, revision resource or practice location.
  • Quiz before turning over. Looking at the solution first turns the card into rereading.
  • Return errors sooner. A simple deck routine can revisit incorrect cards before secure ones without needing a complex system.

In the classroom

A pupil turns a confidently incorrect quiz item into a card. The front contains the question; the back explains the mistake, corrected method and where to find more practice. Later, the pupil attempts the front before turning it over. An incorrect attempt returns sooner in the deck rather than disappearing after the original correction.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will have students turn corrected errors into review cards for later retrieval, so that students revisit and secure the knowledge they got wrong.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Pupils create cards with the question separate from the answer.
  • Cards are used for retrieval later.
  • Errors become less frequent over time.

Quick check of impact

Track whether reviewed errors recur in later assessments.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
