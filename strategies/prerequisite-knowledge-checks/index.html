Prerequisite knowledge checks

Identifying the foundations for new learning

Use this before new teaching when students need specific existing knowledge to access the explanation. Check the actual prerequisite rather than asking whether students remember last lesson.

How to do it

  1. Identify the prerequisite. Name the small piece of knowledge or process that students must retrieve before the new content can make sense.
  2. Elicit it from everyone. Use a short atomised question, mini-whiteboards, call and response or a brief written response. Check competence, not confidence.
  3. State the connection. Explain how the new learning builds on what students already know; highlight similarities and differences.
  4. Start broad, then narrow. Give the general concept or organising idea before introducing detail.
  5. Make students process the link. Use self-explanation, Turn and Talk or a comparison prompt so pupils articulate the connection themselves.
  6. Check meaning and revisit. Probe how ideas connect, address weak links, and return to the concept later so the connection remains accessible.

What it sounds like

“Before we explain ionic formulae, show me the charge on a magnesium ion and a chloride ion. Keep your answers private until the signal.”

Do

  • Gather independent responses before introducing the new demand.
  • Make the connection to new learning explicit.

Avoid

  • Inferring readiness from confident volunteers.
  • Repeating an entire previous lesson when one component needs repair.

Top tips

  • Atomise the check. Test a small prerequisite directly, rather than hiding it inside a complex question that makes failure hard to interpret.
  • Separate recall from application. A remembered definition does not automatically establish that students can use it.
  • Choose a consequential prerequisite. Check the knowledge that would block today’s learning, not everything previously taught.
  • Fix the gap before adding complexity. A brief model and a fresh check may be more useful than continuing over an insecure foundation.
  • Keep the BSCS boundary clear. Spaced retrieval belongs in Do Now; the immediate prerequisite check sits at the opening of Explain.

In the classroom

Before teaching ionic formulae, a Chemistry teacher checks ion charges on boards. Several pupils remember the symbols but confuse charges. The teacher repairs that small prerequisite and checks another pair of ions before modelling charge balance. The new explanation now starts from knowledge the class has shown it can use.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will check one essential prerequisite with every student before new teaching, so that students start the explanation with the knowledge it depends on.

Peer observer look-fors

  • A short, specific prerequisite question is answered by everyone, not just volunteers.
  • The teacher acts on the responses, briefly repairing a gap before moving on.
  • Pupils can use the prerequisite when the new idea is introduced.

Quick check of impact

Count how many pupils get the prerequisite right on first attempt and after repair. Note whether errors in the main task fall.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
