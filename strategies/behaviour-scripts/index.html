Behaviour scripts

Correcting calmly and precisely

Use calm, precise behaviour language to restore learning while preserving relationships. Apply the school behaviour system consistently and keep the response focused on the action that needs to change.

How to do it

  1. Describe and direct. State the observable behaviour and the expected action instead of asking a confrontational ‘why’ question.
  2. Use partial agreement. Acknowledge the pupil’s comment without abandoning the expectation: ‘Maybe so—and the instruction still stands.’
  3. Allow take-up time. Give the direction once, move attention back to teaching and return later to check compliance rather than creating a public stand-off.
  4. Focus on primary behaviour. Address what is interrupting learning and, unless serious, avoid escalating a sigh, mutter or eye-roll that follows the correction.
  5. Offer directed choices. Give two acceptable, behaviour-based options, state the consequence calmly and follow through consistently.

What it sounds like

“You are talking while I am explaining. Stop the conversation and listen to this step. Thank you.”

Do

  • Use brief, predictable language and follow through.
  • Begin with the least intrusive effective response.

Avoid

  • Debating publicly or reacting emotionally.
  • Inventing consequences outside the agreed school system.

Top tips

  • Describe and direct. Name the observable behaviour and the required action, avoiding questions that invite an argument.
  • Allow take-up time. Give a clear direction, move attention away where appropriate, and return to check compliance.
  • Use partial agreement carefully. Acknowledge a point without abandoning the expectation: “Even if you were helping, this is silent work.”
  • Focus on the primary behaviour. Avoid escalating a minor sigh or facial expression while correcting the disruption, unless the secondary behaviour is serious.
  • Make praise precise. Positive narration and recognition should identify the useful learning action rather than publicly rank pupils.

In the classroom

During silent practice, one pupil starts a conversation. The teacher gives a brief description and direction, allows take-up time and resumes monitoring. A quiet return checks that the pupil has begun the task. When another pupil starts promptly, the teacher recognises the specific action. The correction does not become a public debate.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use short, calm, precise correction scripts in line with the behaviour policy, so that students return to learning quickly with minimal disruption.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Corrections are brief, private where possible, and name the expected behaviour.
  • The teacher's tone is calm and the focus returns to learning straight away.
  • Pupils correct their behaviour quickly and the lesson flow is maintained.

Quick check of impact

Note how long corrections take and whether low-level disruption escalates less often.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
