Mini-whiteboards

Checking everyone's understanding

Make every student’s answer visible, identify gaps and decide what to teach next. Useful before independent practice or whenever answers from a few students leave you unsure about everyone else.

How to do it

  1. Plan a revealing question. Target one idea, anticipate plausible wrong answers and choose a word, calculation, sketch or choice that can be scanned.
  2. Rehearse the mechanics. Model clear writing, private answers, boards facing you and erasing only on signal; practise with an easy question.
  3. Secure independent thinking. Give response instructions before the question, allow time and keep boards hidden until commitment is complete.
  4. Collect every response. Reveal together and scan systematically. If revealing back to front, everyone stops writing before the first row shows.
  5. Probe and respond. Look for common errors and missing responses, ask how selected students decided, then Pause & Fix, Adapt & Support or Extend & Challenge as the evidence warrants.

What it sounds like

“One formula, written large. Keep it hidden. Write the formula for magnesium chloride.” [Thinking time.] “Pens down. Show me in three, two, one. Hold still while I check.”

Do

  • Scan every response before giving the answer.
  • Probe reasoning and use a fresh check after repair.

Avoid

  • Accepting the first correct board as evidence for everyone.
  • Using boards to rank students or as punishment.

Top tips

  • Separate working from the answer. Use one side for working and the other for a large final answer; turn it over when explaining the method.
  • Bring bookwork onto boards. Transfer the answer to one pivotal book question onto a board, then scan before showing the solution.
  • Investigate blank boards. Ask what the student knows or which part is unclear; offer a starting prompt and return.
  • Control public answers. Avoid praising an early correct board while others are writing; gather the evidence before revealing the solution.
  • Keep the routine available. Working pens, erasers and boards should be within reach; re-model premature erasing or unreadable answers.

In the classroom

Several Chemistry boards show MgCl rather than MgCl₂. The teacher keeps the responses private until all pupils commit, identifies the pattern and re-models charge balance. Pupils then answer a different ionic formula question. Correcting the original board alone would have shown compliance with the model rather than independent application.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will use mini-whiteboards with a clear routine at key decision points, so that I see every student's answer and adapt teaching to address gaps.

Peer observer look-fors

  • All pupils write and show answers on a clear cue.
  • The teacher scans the boards and responds to errors.
  • Teaching changes based on the responses (for example, reteach, move on or support).

Quick check of impact

Record the proportion of correct boards on the first check and on the re-check.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
