Secure learning through practice · PR04

Fluency practice

Securing the essential steps

Use fluency practice when students understand a process but perform it slowly or inconsistently. Secure accuracy before increasing pace, volume or complexity.

This strategy is consistent with research principles, but the evidence for this kind of practice is less direct, more mixed or more dependent on context.

How to do it

  1. Identify the component. Locate the decision or action that is accurate but hesitant, or repeatedly disrupts the whole task.
  2. Set a short focused practice set. Keep the target clear and begin with familiar forms.
  3. Inspect early attempts. Correct the faulty step before it is repeated and provide a small prompt or model where needed.
  4. Increase efficiency. Reduce prompts and encourage smoother execution while preserving accuracy and explanation.
  5. Recombine and revisit. Use the component in a complete task, then return after a delay and introduce useful variation.

What it sounds like

“For these six items, practise selecting the correct operation. Accuracy comes first. After that decision is secure, we will complete the full problems.”

Do

  • Monitor early items and correct promptly.
  • Build towards accurate independent performance.

Avoid

  • Timed competition before the method is secure.
  • Repeating identical items without attending to their structure.

Top tips

  • Practise the bottleneck. A short set on the difficult component may be more useful than repeatedly completing the whole task.
  • Keep correct models available initially. Students should not spend the practice interval repeatedly rehearsing an error.
  • Increase speed cautiously. Fluency includes accurate, efficient performance; rushing alone is not the aim.
  • Use purposeful variation. Vary features that require attention while keeping the target skill stable.
  • Include delayed practice. Smooth performance immediately after modelling can fade unless revisited.

In the classroom

In Music, students practise a short rhythmic component that interrupts a longer performance. The teacher first secures accuracy, then smoother execution and finally recombination with the full phrase. Later rehearsal revisits the same component. Faster playing is useful only when it preserves the timing and technique being practised.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will provide short, frequent practice of essential steps, prioritising accuracy before speed, so that students perform key procedures fluently and free up thinking for harder tasks.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Practice targets a specific, essential procedure.
  • Accuracy is checked before pace or volume increases.
  • Pupils complete the procedure quickly and correctly.

Quick check of impact

Time and score a short fluency check across several weeks.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
