Everyone–Together–Anyone

Planning participation for the whole class

Use Everyone–Together–Anyone to plan how all students think, rehearse and remain accountable. Choose a participation sequence that serves the learning rather than rely on confident volunteers.

How to do it

  1. Everyone thinks. Begin with silent solo thinking or Everybody Writes when the question requires an original response.
  2. Together rehearses. Give partners a precise prompt, a short time limit and a listening expectation.
  3. Anyone shares. Cold call individuals to report their own answer, their partner’s reasoning or a refined joint response.
  4. Everyone remains accountable. Invite pupils to compare, restate, improve or show a final independent response.

What it sounds like

“Everyone write a first explanation. Together, compare the evidence with your partner. Anyone may then be asked to explain which idea you kept and why.”

Do

  • Plan the cognitive action for every pupil.
  • Keep participation and diagnosis distinct.

Avoid

  • Volunteer-only questioning.
  • Treating visible participation as proof of understanding.

Top tips

  • Begin with Everyone. An independent thought, jot or board response prevents the first speaker supplying everyone’s answer.
  • Make Together purposeful. Partner talk should compare, explain or refine the thinking generated first.
  • Use Anyone for accountability. Select a prepared student to share and involve listeners in evaluating the response.
  • Choose the sequence for the task. Brief recall may use a different combination from extended reasoning; the framework is not a compulsory three-stage script.
  • Inspect thinking as well as activity. Busy talk or moving pens can still hide copied or shallow responses.

In the classroom

A lesson first asks every pupil to write a proposed explanation. Partner talk then compares the evidence, and selected pupils share their reasoning. The teacher follows with an individual check where diagnosis matters. The participation sequence involves everyone without confusing a successful discussion with proof of each pupil’s independent understanding.

Use it for Individual Development

Example goal

I will plan when students respond individually, together or by being selected, so that all students think and participate throughout the lesson.

Peer observer look-fors

  • Participation methods are planned and varied.
  • All pupils are accountable for responding.
  • Few pupils remain passive.

Quick check of impact

Observe the proportion of pupils actively responding at key moments.

Adapt the goal to your class and subject. See writing a precise goal and peer observation.
