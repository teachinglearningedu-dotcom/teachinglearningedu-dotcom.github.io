Signalling for attention and giving instructions

Prime students before the transition

Before stopping students, the teacher gives a short warning that full attention will be needed e.g. “In 30 seconds, I will need pens down and eyes this way.”

  • 2. Teacher moves to a visible position

The teacher stands where they can see the whole class and the whole class can see them. This may be front centre / front side of the room

  • 3. Teachers use the whole-school signal

"3 - no talking.2 – hands free, eyes this way.1 - Focus ready.Thank you.”

  • 4. Students are “Focus Ready”

"Focus ready” means:

Silence

Equipment on table

Hands still and free (no equipment in hand)

Facing the teacher and eyes on teacher/ board

Track the teacher throughout the instruction/ explanation

Ready to listen, think and respond

  • 5. Staff are to “Be Seen Looking” and positively narrate compliance

After the countdown, the teacher pauses deliberately and scans the room.e.g. “Thank you, for having your pens down and being Focus Ready.”

  • 6. If required, use least invasive correction

If some students are not ready, the teacher first uses a brief anonymous correction.e.g. “Just waiting for two to have hands free and eyes this way.”

  • 7. If further required, correct precisely and use behaviour system as needed

If attention is still not secured after least invasive correction the teacher gives a named what-to-do correction and to use the consequence system (Name, correct the behaviour, consequence) e.g. “Name, You need to be Focus Ready as it is disrupting the learning for yourself and others. I am giving you a Warning. Thank you.”

  • 8. Teachers deliberately pause and begin only when 100% attention is secured

e.g. “Thank you. I have everyone. We are all now ready”

Why?

Ensures teachers can secure full attention before instructions, explanations, transitions or feedback. This supports calm classrooms, reduces repeated teacher talk, protects learning time and ensures students know exactly what to stop, where to look and how to listen.

Signalling for attention routine

Giving instructions

1.Clear concise What to do instructions given and front-load means of participation

Give clear, effective directions to students that are specific, concrete, sequential and observable.

“When I say go and not before I need you to answer the six questions on the board. This is your chance to show how much you have learnt from our I do and We do stages and impress me with your knowledge.”

  • 2. Check instructions

Depending on the complexity of instructions, this could be multiple students being checked, using name at end to increase the attention e.g. “What is the task you need to do….[student name]?” “What is the volume required of you for this task…[student name]?” How long have you got…[student name]?”

  • 3. Set students off

Use an extended pause to build anticipation and authority

“Five minutes, on your own, in absolute silence. Ready… [extended pause]...Go”

  • 3. Teacher moves to a visible position to “Be Seen Looking”

Actively scan the classroom for the first 30 seconds of a task to confirm students are following expectations and know that you are checking

  • 4. Then teacher to circulate to support and carry out active observation

Teacher to use their circulation route to check in with students and to actively observe to feed into feedback if independent practice

Explanations

Students to remain Focus Ready throughout to secure their attention:

No talking

Equipment on table

Hands still and free (no equipment in hand)

Facing the teacher and eyes on teacher/ board

Track the teacher throughout the instruction/ explanation

Ready to listen, think and respond
