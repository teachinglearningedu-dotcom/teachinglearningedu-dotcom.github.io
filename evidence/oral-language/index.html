Evidence summary

Oral language interventions

ALIASES: dialogic teaching; structured classroom talk; vocabulary talk; speaking and listening; purposeful questioning

BSCS COMPONENT: Drive thinking for all; Explain; Check and Adapt; Climate and culture

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +6 months average impact; very low cost; high evidence security; 188 studies; Toolkit review updated May 2025.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence for oral-language approaches. The evidence base is strongest for literacy and reading-comprehension outcomes.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students give short or imprecise explanations, do not use subject vocabulary, cannot articulate connections, participate unevenly in discussion, or fail to listen and respond to others' ideas.

MECHANISM: Purposeful spoken interaction supports vocabulary, explanation, connection-making and metacognitive awareness. Adults model and scaffold high-quality speaking and listening while talk is integrated with curriculum content.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

The EEF reports a high average impact, with the strongest evidence for approaches integrated into the curriculum.

Interaction between teacher and pupils and between peers is a central feature.

Vocabulary teaching is stronger when words are connected to current curriculum content and used actively and meaningfully.

Evidence is mainly based on literacy and reading-comprehension outcomes; the impact on science and mathematics attainment is less clear.

Professional development and deliberate design are important; merely increasing the amount of talk is not the intervention.

DO

Set a precise question, thinking time, speaking structure and intended product.

Model the language, reasoning and listening behaviours pupils are expected to use.

Require active use of essential subject vocabulary in meaningful explanations.

Structure accountability so every pupil thinks, speaks, listens and can report the pair's idea.

Follow discussion with an independent check when individual understanding matters.

DON'T

Treat any pupil conversation as evidence-informed oral-language practice.

Use open-ended pair talk without thinking time, roles, time limits or an outcome.

Allow confident pupils to dominate while quieter or lower-prior-attaining pupils remain passive.

Assume verbal fluency is the same as secure subject understanding.

Generalise the +6-month literacy-dominated estimate to a single Turn and Talk in any subject.

AGENT USE: Use this as the strongest EEF evidence link for well-structured Turn and Talk, vocabulary rehearsal and dialogic questioning. Cold Call can help sample subsequent understanding but is not itself evaluated by this Toolkit strand.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Oral language interventions; Guidance: Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools
