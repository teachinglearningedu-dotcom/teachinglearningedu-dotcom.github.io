Evidence summary

Explicit instruction, guided practice and scaffolding

BSCS COMPONENT: Explain; Secure learning through practice; Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt

BSCS COMPONENT: Explain; Secure learning through practice; Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF guidance-based evidence from the SEND five-a-day and metacognition guidance. There is no general EEF Toolkit strand called explicit instruction.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Explanations are too large or unclear, pupils imitate procedures without understanding decisions, independent practice begins before readiness, or support is not faded.

EEF SUMMARY: EEF describes explicit instruction as teacher demonstration followed by guided and independent practice. Effective scaffolding is temporary, targeted to the current barrier and removed as competence increases.

DO

Sequence content into coherent steps and connect each step to prerequisite knowledge.

Model both the procedure and the thinking that guides expert decisions.

Use guided practice with frequent checks before releasing pupils to independent work.

Select prompts, clues, models, worked steps or vocabulary support to match the barrier.

Plan how support will be reduced and verify independent success.

DON'T

Equate explicit instruction with uninterrupted teacher talk.

Model every action while leaving the important decisions implicit.

Move to independent practice because the teacher has finished explaining.

Give all pupils the same scaffold regardless of need.

Leave scaffolds in place until they become part of the task rather than temporary support.

AGENT USE: Link BSCS I do - We do - You do, thinking aloud, guided practice and scaffolding to EEF guidance. Do not invent a standalone EEF impact estimate for explicit instruction.

Primary EEF sources: EEF: Five-a-day for pupils with SEND; Guidance: Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning
