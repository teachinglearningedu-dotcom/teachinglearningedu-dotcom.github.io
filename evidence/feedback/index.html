Evidence summary

Feedback

ALIASES: live feedback; whole-class feedback; verbal feedback; written feedback; self-correction; redrafting; Show Call

BSCS COMPONENT: Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice; Feedback to improve; Lesson preparation

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +6 months average impact; very low cost; high evidence security; 155 studies; Toolkit review updated June 2021.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence, supported by Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning (2021) and the 2026 Check. Adapt. practitioner tool.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students repeat errors, cannot see the gap between current and successful performance, ignore comments, do not improve work, or wait passively for teacher correction.

MECHANISM: Information about current performance redirects either teacher or pupil action towards a clear learning goal. Impact depends on strong initial teaching, evidence of the learning gap, appropriately timed information and pupils actually using it.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

Feedback is well evidenced, but not all feedback improves learning and poorly designed feedback can have negative effects.

Task, subject and self-regulation feedback tends to be more useful than comments about personal traits.

The principles matter more than whether feedback is written, verbal, digital, individual or whole class.

EEF guidance places high-quality instruction and formative assessment before the feedback itself.

Teachers should plan how pupils will receive and use feedback; comments without pupil action do not close the loop.

DO

Anchor feedback to a clear learning intention, model or success criterion.

Identify the most important gap and give information that enables a feasible next action.

Choose timing and method according to the task, pupil and learning need.

Protect time for correction, redrafting, rehearsal or fresh practice.

Use whole-class or live feedback when it is more proportionate and instructionally useful than extensive marking.

DON'T

Confuse the quantity of marking with the quality of feedback.

Give vague praise, grades alone or comments focused on personal characteristics.

Provide so many targets that attention is dispersed and action becomes unlikely.

Use feedback to compensate for unclear explanation, weak modelling or a poorly designed task.

Specify one universal feedback method or frequency regardless of context.

AGENT USE: For Show Call, whole-class feedback or live feedback, describe the technique as a delivery method aligned with EEF feedback principles. Do not claim that the EEF independently assigns +6 months to that named classroom routine.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Feedback; Guidance: Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning; EEF: Check. Adapt.
