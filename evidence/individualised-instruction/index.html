Evidence summary

Individualised instruction

ALIASES: personalised tasks; adaptive software; individual pacing; targeted individual support

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +4 months average impact; very low cost; limited evidence security; 198 studies; Toolkit review updated July 2021.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence, but this approach should not be treated as synonymous with BSCS adaptive teaching.

STUDENT PROBLEM: A pupil has a precisely identified gap or needs closely matched practice and feedback, especially where digital or targeted support can supplement normal teaching.

MECHANISM: Assessment identifies an individual learning need; activity, pace or feedback is matched to that need; progress is monitored; and targeted instruction supplements strong teacher interaction.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

The average impact is positive but there is substantial variation and limited evidence security.

Individualised instruction can be organisationally demanding and may reduce high-quality teacher interaction if the teacher becomes mainly a manager of separate activities.

Effects are somewhat higher in secondary settings, where pupils may have stronger self-regulation.

Digital technology can support assessment and feedback, but should supplement rather than replace teaching.

DO

Begin with an accurate diagnosis of the pupil's knowledge, skill or barrier.

Use targeted individual activity as a supplement to high-quality whole-class instruction.

Match the task closely to current knowledge and provide timely feedback.

Monitor whether the approach increases learning rather than only task completion.

Prefer inclusive whole-class or temporary small-group adaptation when it can address the need efficiently.

DON'T

Create a different worksheet for every pupil by default.

Equate adaptive teaching with permanent individualised curricula or lowered expectations.

Allow management of separate tasks to displace teacher explanation and interaction.

Assume an adaptive digital platform is effective without monitoring learning.

Use fixed labels to determine pace or ceiling.

AGENT USE: Use cautiously for a specific diagnosed need. The preferred BSCS default is ambitious common learning with precise scaffolding, flexible grouping and responsive adaptation, not routine individualisation.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Individualised instruction; EEF: Five-a-day for pupils with SEND
