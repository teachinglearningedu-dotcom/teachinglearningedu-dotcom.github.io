Evidence summary

Metacognition and self-regulation

ALIASES: thinking aloud; plan-monitor-evaluate; self-questioning; metacognitive talk; gradual independence

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Drive thinking for all; Check and Adapt; Explain; Secure learning through practice; Feedback to improve

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +8 months average impact; very low cost; high evidence security; 355 studies; Toolkit review updated May 2025.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence, supported by the EEF Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning guidance report, second edition (November 2025).

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students cannot explain their reasoning, select an approach, monitor progress, recover from error, transfer a method, or work independently without repeated teacher prompting.

MECHANISM: Pupils are explicitly taught how to plan, monitor and evaluate learning in a subject. Teachers model the hidden decisions of expert performance, prompt purposeful self-questioning and fade support as pupils become more independent.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

The Toolkit reports a high average impact, but also warns that the impact can be difficult to realise in ordinary practice.

Effective approaches explicitly teach strategies within normal curriculum content rather than as generic thinking-skills lessons.

Thinking aloud, worked examples, success criteria, reflection questions and structured reasoning can make expert processes visible.

Challenge needs to be sufficient for pupils to use a strategy, but not so great that the strategy cannot be applied successfully.

The 2025 guidance emphasises curriculum-embedded strategy instruction, scaffolding, metacognitive talk and planned movement towards independence.

DO

Model the cue, decision, monitoring check and correction - not just the visible steps.

Teach a small number of subject-specific plan-monitor-evaluate prompts and apply them to authentic tasks.

Use structured talk so pupils articulate why an approach works and how they know it is working.

Fade prompts, checklists and teacher narration once pupils show secure independent use.

Check whether pupils can select and use the strategy without being reminded.

DON'T

Teach generic 'learning to learn' activities detached from subject knowledge.

Label reflection as metacognition when pupils merely state whether they enjoyed or completed a task.

Overload pupils with long checklists or prompts that become another task to manage.

Assume that a teacher think-aloud is effective when it narrates actions but hides decisions.

Keep scaffolds in place after pupils can perform successfully without them.

AGENT USE: Recommend this when the learning problem concerns reasoning, transfer, self-correction or independence. Techniques such as thinking aloud and structured self-questioning are directly aligned. Do not attach the +8-month estimate to a single think-aloud routine.
