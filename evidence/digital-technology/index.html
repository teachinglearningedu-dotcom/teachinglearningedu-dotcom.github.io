Evidence summary

Using digital technology to improve learning

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Explain; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice; Feedback to improve

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Explain; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice; Feedback to improve

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF guidance-based evidence. Technology is not treated as a generic high-impact strategy; impact depends on how it improves pedagogy.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Practice or feedback is too infrequent, pupils need improved access or representation, assessment information is slow or inaccurate, or technology adds distraction and task complexity.

EEF SUMMARY: EEF guidance focuses on whether technology improves teaching and learning - for example through better modelling, assessment, feedback, access or the quality and quantity of practice - rather than whether devices are present.

DO

Identify the pedagogical problem before choosing the tool.

Use technology where it improves modelling, access, practice, assessment or feedback.

Teach the routine and reduce unnecessary clicks, transitions and split attention.

Monitor the accuracy, independence and learning produced by adaptive platforms.

Plan staff development and implementation support.

DON'T

Assume technology improves learning because it increases engagement or novelty.

Use digital activities that replace thinking with clicking, copying or guessing.

Adopt a platform without checking curriculum alignment and feedback quality.

Ignore access, accessibility, privacy or workload barriers.

Replace high-quality teacher interaction with automated individualisation by default.

AGENT USE: Recommend technology only as an enabling feature of a clearly specified teaching strategy. Do not attach a generic EEF effect size to technology use.

Primary EEF sources: Guidance: Using Digital Technology to Improve Learning
