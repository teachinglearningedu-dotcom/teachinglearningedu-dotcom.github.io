Evidence summary

Deployment of teaching assistants

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice; Explain

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice; Explain

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF guidance-based evidence. The current guidance is the third edition, published March 2025.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Pupils with SEND receive less teacher interaction, a teaching assistant becomes the default adult, support creates dependency, or TAs lack curriculum information and a shared plan.

EEF SUMMARY: EEF guidance states that TAs should enable access to high-quality teaching and supplement rather than replace the teacher. Structured scaffolding should move from the least intensive support towards modelling or correction only when needed, then back towards pupil independence.

DO

Plan the teacher-TA partnership, intended learning, likely barriers and routes for checking.

Ensure pupils supported by TAs retain access to teacher explanation and feedback.

Use a scaffolding sequence such as self-scaffolding, prompting, clueing, modelling and correcting.

Share resources and essential subject knowledge early enough for purposeful preparation.

Evaluate independence and learning, not simply whether work was completed.

DON'T

Use the TA as a permanent substitute teacher for pupils with the greatest needs.

Seat a pupil with a TA for every lesson without reviewing the effect on independence and belonging.

Prompt so quickly that the pupil does not think or retrieve for themselves.

Judge success by neat or completed work when understanding remains insecure.

Ask TAs to improvise unfamiliar explanations without shared planning.

AGENT USE: When recommending TA support, preserve teacher responsibility and specify how support will be faded. Avoid 'TA to help' as a complete strategy.

Primary EEF sources: Guidance: Deployment of Teaching Assistants
