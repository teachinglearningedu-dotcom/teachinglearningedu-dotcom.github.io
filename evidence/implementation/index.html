Implementation of evidence-informed strategies

BSCS COMPONENT: All BSCS components; Strand 3 Individual Development; professional development

BSCS COMPONENT: All BSCS components; Strand 3 Individual Development; professional development

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF implementation guidance, third edition, published April 2024. This evidence concerns how schools select and embed approaches rather than the instructional impact of a strategy itself.

STUDENT PROBLEM: A promising strategy is reduced to a label, implemented inconsistently, abandoned too quickly, or evaluated only through teacher activity rather than student response and learning.

EEF SUMMARY: EEF implementation guidance highlights the behaviours that drive implementation, contextual factors that enable it and a structured process. For an individual teacher, this means defining the problem, selecting a plausible approach, specifying active ingredients, using support and monitoring, and refining without losing the critical features.

DO

Begin with a clear student learning problem and a plausible mechanism.

Specify the strategy's critical features, examples and non-examples.

Keep the first change manageable and provide modelling, rehearsal and follow-up.

Monitor both implementation and what students do differently.

Adapt peripheral features to context while preserving the active ingredients.

DON'T

Select a strategy only because its Toolkit impact headline is large.

Launch a long list of techniques without sufficient depth or follow-up.

Judge success by whether a teacher used the routine once.

Change the critical features until the strategy no longer reflects the evidence.

Treat variation in outcomes as proof that evidence or professional judgement is irrelevant.

AGENT USE: Use implementation guidance to shape the Word briefing's recommended starting point and first practice priority. Do not turn a quick strategy request into the full Strand 3 enquiry unless the user asks.

Primary EEF sources: Guidance: A School's Guide to Implementation
