High-quality teaching for pupils with SEND: EEF Five-a-day

BSCS COMPONENT: All BSCS components, particularly Lesson preparation; Explain; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice

BSCS COMPONENT: All BSCS components, particularly Lesson preparation; Explain; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF guidance-based evidence. The five approaches are not a single Toolkit strand with one months-of-progress estimate.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Pupils face barriers linked to prior knowledge, language, executive function, sensory processing, motor development or social and emotional needs, while the learning ambition should remain shared.

EEF SUMMARY: EEF identifies five broadly useful approaches that particularly support many pupils with SEND: explicit instruction; cognitive and metacognitive strategies; scaffolding; flexible grouping; and purposeful use of technology.

DO

Start from the barrier and retain an ambitious shared learning goal.

Use clear modelling, guided practice and independent practice within explicit instruction.

Choose the lightest useful verbal, visual or written scaffold and plan its removal.

Form temporary groups from current evidence and disband them when the purpose is met.

Use technology only when it improves access, recording, practice or feedback.

DON'T

Turn a SEND label into a fixed prediction of what a pupil can learn.

Create permanently easier work or a separate curriculum by default.

Keep pupils in fixed ability groups when current evidence has changed.

Over-scaffold until the pupil completes the task without doing the thinking.

Treat technology or a teaching assistant as a substitute for teacher responsibility.

AGENT USE: This is the default EEF source for inclusive high-quality teaching. Use the specific five-a-day approach that matches the barrier. Do not state an impact month estimate for the five-a-day cluster.

Primary EEF sources: Guidance: Special Educational Needs in Mainstream Schools; EEF: Five-a-day for pupils with SEND
