Evidence

How we rate evidence

Know what supports the advice, and how far the claim can travel.

Every strategy page shows how strong the evidence is for that kind of practice. The rating is a guide to how much weight the evidence can bear, not a promise that a strategy will work in every classroom.

How strategies are rated

RatingWhat it meansStrategies
Strong evidence baseThis strategy applies an approach with a strong, consistent research base, such as high-security EEF evidence or well-replicated findings from cognitive science. Research on this exact routine may be more limited, so check how your pupils respond.13
Research-informedThis strategy is consistent with research principles, but the evidence for this kind of practice is less direct, more mixed or more dependent on context.46
Established practiceThis strategy is mainly based on expert practitioner experience and agreed BSCS routines. Research supports the underlying principles indirectly.21

Core strategies

Core strategies combine the strongest evidence with the BSCS teaching model's priorities. They are a good place to start, but your choice should still come from what your pupils need. There are 23 core strategies.

Using evidence well

  • EEF Toolkit estimates (for example, "+6 months") describe broad approaches across many studies. They are never an effect size for a single classroom routine such as cold calling or a Do Now.
  • Evidence describes what has tended to work on average. Implementation, subject and pupils all matter, so check how your pupils respond.
  • School agreement and practitioner advice are useful, but they are not research findings. Each page states its source and status.
