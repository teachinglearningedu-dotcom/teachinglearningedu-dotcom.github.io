Evidence summary

Behaviour interventions

ALIASES: classroom management; universal behaviour approaches; targeted behaviour support; routines and rehearsal

BSCS COMPONENT: Climate and culture; Lesson preparation

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +3 months average impact; low cost; moderate evidence security; 83 studies; Toolkit review updated May 2025.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence for behaviour interventions, supported by Improving Behaviour in Schools (2019). It does not directly evaluate every BSCS routine.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Disruption reduces learning time, classroom expectations are inconsistent, routines are insecure, pupils are disengaged, or universal support is insufficient for a smaller number of pupils.

MECHANISM: Universal and targeted approaches reduce disruption and increase engaged learning time. Consistency, explicit teaching and rehearsal, high expectations and staff development influence implementation quality.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

Both universal and targeted approaches show positive overall effects; schools usually need an appropriate combination.

Approaches with an academic component or role-play and rehearsal show higher impacts in the evidence base.

Implementation consistency and professional development are important.

If an intervention consumes more learning time than the disruption it replaces, engaged learning time may not improve.

DO

Teach, model, rehearse and reinforce a small number of clear routines.

Align classroom practice with consistent whole-school expectations.

Maintain high expectations while tailoring targeted support to identified need.

Monitor learning time, participation and academic engagement as well as incident counts.

Use calm, predictable responses and preserve pupil dignity.

DON'T

Introduce isolated gimmicks without consistent implementation.

Treat behaviour as separate from academic success, task design and belonging.

Lower expectations for pupils with SEND or disadvantaged pupils.

Use a time-intensive intervention that displaces more learning than it protects.

Claim that EEF directly tested a countdown, threshold greeting or named TLAC routine.

AGENT USE: Use this as the broad evidence base for Climate and culture. Attribute specific routines to BSCS policy and practitioner guidance, while describing them as consistent with EEF principles on routines, rehearsal and consistency.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Behaviour interventions; Guidance: Improving Behaviour in Schools

These entries are important for BSCS practice but do not carry one generic Toolkit effect-size estimate. The agent should describe them as EEF guidance-based or EEF-informed and avoid invented months-of-progress claims.
