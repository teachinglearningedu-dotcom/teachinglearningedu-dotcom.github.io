Evidence summary

Reading comprehension strategies

ALIASES: activating schema; predicting; clarifying; inference; summarising; self-questioning; graphic organisers

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Explain; Drive thinking for all; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +7 months average impact; very low cost; moderate evidence security; 184 studies; Toolkit review updated October 2025.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence. The BSCS link is strongest where pupils must understand subject texts, questions, source material or written explanations.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students can decode words but do not understand a text, miss key information, struggle to infer or summarise, fail to connect text to prior knowledge, or cannot select a useful reading strategy.

MECHANISM: Expert reading processes are modelled and explicitly taught, then practised through guided and independent reading until pupils can select strategies for themselves.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

The Toolkit reports a high average impact across primary and secondary settings and across a range of subjects.

Successful approaches diagnose the barrier and match the text and strategy to pupils' reading capabilities.

Explicit modelling, guided practice and gradual release are important; a worksheet naming strategies is not sufficient.

Background knowledge and vocabulary strongly affect comprehension, especially for disadvantaged pupils and older struggling readers.

Pupils may struggle because of decoding, fluency, vocabulary, syntax, background knowledge or comprehension; the same response will not fit every cause.

DO

Identify the precise comprehension demand of the subject text or question.

Model how an expert reader notices cues, asks questions, clarifies and checks meaning.

Activate only the prior knowledge that is genuinely needed and pre-teach essential vocabulary.

Choose texts that are challenging enough to require the strategy but still accessible.

Move from explicit teaching to guided practice and then independent application across contexts.

DON'T

Assume every weak written response is caused by weak comprehension.

Use generic reading-strategy worksheets without modelling the thinking involved.

Ask pupils to predict or highlight without making the learning purpose clear.

Overload a short text with too many simultaneous comprehension prompts.

Keep pupils dependent on an organiser once they can comprehend independently.

AGENT USE: Recommend this for problems involving subject reading, command words, inference, extracting evidence or interpreting extended questions. Distinguish comprehension from decoding and vocabulary problems before selecting a technique.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Reading comprehension strategies; Guidance: Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools
