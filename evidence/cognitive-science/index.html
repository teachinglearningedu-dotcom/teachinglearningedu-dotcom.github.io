Evidence summary

Cognitive science approaches: retrieval, spacing, cognitive load, worked examples and dual coding

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Do Now; Explain; Secure learning through practice

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Do Now; Explain; Secure learning through practice

EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION: EEF evidence-and-practice review, not a Toolkit strand. The review explicitly warns that applied classroom evidence is limited and context dependent.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students forget previously taught content, working memory is overloaded, examples do not support novice learning, or visuals distract rather than clarify.

EEF SUMMARY: The 2021 EEF review found promise for retrieval practice, spacing and worked examples, while emphasising that classroom evidence is limited. Interleaving is mostly tested in mathematics, and dual coding can produce null or negative effects when irrelevant images add cognitive load.

DO

Space retrieval of important knowledge over time and provide correction or responsive review.

Use retrieval that requires pupils to recall, not copy, and select content with a clear curriculum purpose.

Chunk complex information and use worked examples or scaffolds when prior knowledge is limited.

Coordinate words and visuals so each representation contributes to understanding.

Adapt the approach to subject, age, prior knowledge and the intended learning outcome.

DON'T

Present cognitive-science principles as silver bullets or universal prescriptions.

Turn every Do Now into an unplanned quiz of the most recent lesson.

Add decorative images and call the slide dual coded.

Interleave before pupils have enough knowledge to discriminate between problem types.

Assume recall of a fact demonstrates connected understanding or transfer.

AGENT USE: Use this source for Do Now retrieval, chunking, worked examples, reduced redundancy and dual coding. State that the technique is informed by EEF's cognitive-science review and include its classroom-evidence limitations.
