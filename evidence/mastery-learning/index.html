Evidence summary

Mastery learning

ALIASES: high success before progression; diagnostic assessment; additional time and support; corrective teaching

BSCS COMPONENT: Lesson preparation; Check and Adapt; Secure learning through practice

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +5 months average impact; very low cost; low evidence security; 80 studies; Toolkit review updated September 2021.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence for mastery-learning systems. This is broader than maintaining a high success rate within one lesson.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students move on with insecure prerequisite knowledge, repeated errors accumulate, independent practice begins before readiness, or some pupils need additional time and support to reach a shared outcome.

MECHANISM: Learning is sequenced into clear outcomes; understanding is diagnosed; pupils receive timely corrective support; and progression follows a high level of success rather than elapsed time alone.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

EEF reports a positive average impact but substantial variation and challenging implementation.

Successful approaches often set a demanding mastery threshold, commonly around 80-90%, while providing additional support to those not yet secure.

Effects are lower on average in secondary than primary studies and stronger in mathematics and science than reading.

The strand is distinct from the broader label 'teaching for mastery'.

DO

Specify the knowledge or process that must be secure before the next step.

Use diagnostic checks and plan corrective explanation, examples and practice.

Vary support and time while preserving a shared ambitious outcome.

Use an 80% success guide as evidence for a decision, not as an inflexible classroom law.

Provide productive extension while others receive corrective support.

DON'T

Move on simply because planned time has elapsed when core understanding is absent.

Confuse mastery with pupils working through worksheets at their own pace.

Use a rigid threshold without examining which errors matter.

Lower the learning goal for pupils who need more time or scaffolding.

Claim that a single high-success practice task is a tested mastery-learning programme.

AGENT USE: Use mastery evidence when the problem concerns readiness, accumulated gaps, pace or transition to independent practice. Describe BSCS high-success and Check-Adapt routines as aligned components, not the whole evaluated approach.
