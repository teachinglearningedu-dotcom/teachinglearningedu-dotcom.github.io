Evidence summary

Evidence, attribution and professional judgement

Prepared for use alongside the BSCS Teaching Strategy Knowledge Base and Teaching and Learning Policy

Prepared for use alongside the BSCS Teaching Strategy Knowledge Base and Teaching and Learning Policy

1. Purpose and retrieval rules

This document is a retrieval source for the BSCS Strategy Adviser. It helps the agent select relevant evidence, explain why a strategy may help, generate accurate Do and Don't guidance, and create a concise Word briefing without misrepresenting the Education Endowment Foundation (EEF).

Core rule: Treat EEF findings as contextual best bets, not guarantees. Match evidence to the student learning problem, plausible cause, BSCS component and implementation conditions.

Evidence attribution levels

DIRECT EEF TOOLKIT EVIDENCE - the EEF Toolkit evaluates the named broad approach and reports average impact, cost and evidence security.

EEF GUIDANCE-BASED - an EEF guidance report recommends or exemplifies the practice, but no standalone Toolkit impact estimate applies.

EEF-ALIGNED PRACTICE - a BSCS or practitioner technique reflects mechanisms in broader EEF evidence, but the exact named routine was not directly evaluated by the EEF.

BSCS OR PRACTITIONER GUIDANCE - the technique is supported by school policy, DistilED, Tips for Teachers, TLAC, StepLab or professional synthesis. Do not relabel it as direct EEF evidence.

How to interpret Toolkit headlines

Months of additional progress are average estimates from research comparisons, not a prediction for a BSCS class or a guarantee from a single lesson.

Evidence security describes confidence in the research base. It is not the same as impact: a positive average can still rest on limited or highly variable evidence.

Cost estimates concern additional implementation costs and do not capture all staff time, training or opportunity costs.

A Toolkit strand usually covers a broad family of approaches. Its headline must not be attached to one micro-technique unless the source directly supports that claim.

The agent should look beyond the headline to implementation detail, age, subject, outcome, disadvantage, feasibility and likely mechanism.

Use the teacher's professional judgement. Baseline evidence may refine a recommendation but is not a condition for receiving useful strategy advice.

Selection rules for the Strategy Adviser

Start with the observable student problem, not the strategy label.

Select no more than three to five best bets for a problem-specific briefing.

Prioritise direct match and plausible mechanism over the largest months-of-progress figure.

Distinguish direct EEF evidence from EEF-aligned BSCS techniques in every briefing.

State an EEF impact estimate only when quoting the broad Toolkit approach and include evidence-security and context caveats.

For each recommendation, specify critical features, Do, Don't, an example, an inclusive adaptation and how the teacher can check whether it is working.

Recommend the smallest manageable starting change and one first practice priority.

2. BSCS component to EEF evidence map

Lesson preparation: SEND five-a-day; cognitive science; metacognition; literacy; teaching-assistant deployment; implementation. Plan the learning goal, prerequisite knowledge, likely barriers, participation, models, checks and adult deployment.

Climate and culture: Behaviour interventions and behaviour guidance; oral-language participation conditions; SEND inclusion. Create safe, predictable and high-expectation conditions for attention, error and participation.

Drive thinking for all: Oral language; metacognition; collaborative learning; peer tutoring; Check. Adapt. Use structures that make every pupil think and provide valid evidence, not volunteer-only participation.

Check and Adapt: Feedback; Check. Adapt.; mastery learning; SEND scaffolding and flexible grouping. Gather representative evidence, interpret the pattern and make a precise next move.

1 Do Now: Cognitive science retrieval and spacing; metacognition; homework only where work is completed outside lessons. No direct EEF effect size belongs to the named BSCS Do Now routine.

2 Explain: Explicit instruction; metacognition; cognitive science; oral language; literacy; digital technology. Sequence, model, manage load, teach vocabulary and check active processing.

3 Secure learning through practice: Mastery learning; explicit instruction/guided practice; scaffolding; feedback; peer tutoring; individualised instruction as a supplement. Check readiness and move towards independent accuracy.

4 Feedback to improve: Feedback; metacognition; peer tutoring/peer assessment; Check. Adapt. Ensure information is actionable and pupils use it to improve.

Authority: BSCS policy defines what is expected at BSCS. EEF evidence informs why an approach may be a good bet. DistilED and Tips for Teachers add implementation detail. The agent must keep these source roles distinct.

5. Named BSCS technique attribution map

Use this section when a teacher asks for a named routine. It prevents a broad EEF headline from being attached to an exact technique that the EEF did not directly evaluate.

Do Now and retrieval practice

BEST EEF LINK: Cognitive science evidence review; metacognition where pupils plan or monitor retrieval; homework only for work completed outside lessons.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned. No EEF Toolkit impact estimate applies to the named BSCS Do Now routine.

Mini-whiteboards, ABCD cards and hinge questions

BEST EEF LINK: Feedback guidance; Check. Adapt.; formative assessment principles.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned checking techniques. Their value depends on valid evidence and a responsive next action.

Cold Call

BEST EEF LINK: Check. Adapt.; oral-language and participation principles; BSCS practitioner guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned, not directly evaluated as a named EEF intervention.

Call and Response

BEST EEF LINK: Oral-language principles and cognitive-science retrieval; BSCS practitioner guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned for precise rehearsal or listening checks; not a direct proxy for understanding.

Turn and Talk

BEST EEF LINK: Oral-language interventions; metacognitive talk; collaborative learning where the task is genuinely collaborative.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned. Do not attach collaborative-learning or oral-language months to one brief paired exchange.

Thinking aloud

BEST EEF LINK: Directly supported within metacognition guidance and reading-comprehension evidence.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: Strong direct alignment when the teacher models decisions, monitoring and self-correction.

Explicit vocabulary instruction

BEST EEF LINK: Oral-language Toolkit; reading comprehension; secondary literacy guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: Strong direct alignment when vocabulary is curriculum-linked and actively used.

Chunking and reducing redundancy

BEST EEF LINK: Cognitive-science review; explicit-instruction and SEND guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-informed. Applied classroom evidence is context dependent and has no single effect-size estimate.

Dual coding

BEST EEF LINK: Cognitive-science review.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-informed with a strong caution: relevant representations can help, while decorative or redundant images can increase load.

Guided practice and scaffolding

BEST EEF LINK: SEND five-a-day; metacognition guidance; mastery-learning components.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: Strong guidance alignment. A single scaffold is not a separately evaluated Toolkit intervention.

Flexible grouping

BEST EEF LINK: SEND five-a-day; collaborative learning where there is a shared group task.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF guidance-based. Groups should be temporary, purposeful and based on current evidence.

Show Call and whole-class feedback

BEST EEF LINK: Feedback Toolkit and guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: EEF-aligned delivery methods. Impact depends on the information, learning goal and pupil action, not display alone.

Structured peer assessment

BEST EEF LINK: Feedback; peer tutoring.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: Directly related evidence, but only when criteria, roles, accuracy checks and improvement action are trained and monitored.

Attention signals, threshold and 100% routines

BEST EEF LINK: Behaviour interventions and behaviour guidance; BSCS policy and practitioner guidance.

SAFE ATTRIBUTION: Broadly EEF-aligned through consistency, rehearsal and protected learning time; not directly evaluated as named routines.
