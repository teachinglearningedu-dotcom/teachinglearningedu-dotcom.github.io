Evidence summary

Peer tutoring

ALIASES: reciprocal tutoring; peer explanation; trained peer support; structured peer assessment

BSCS COMPONENT: Drive thinking for all; Secure learning through practice; Feedback to improve

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +6 months average impact; very low cost; high evidence security; 145 studies; Toolkit review updated May 2025.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence for structured peer tutoring, not for every occasion when pupils help or assess one another.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Students need additional consolidation, explanation or feedback; some pupils benefit from rehearsing knowledge by teaching it; peer assessment is superficial or inaccurate.

MECHANISM: Learners take defined responsibility for explicit teaching support and evaluation. Both tutor and tutee can benefit when roles, materials and feedback processes are trained and monitored.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

Peer tutoring tends to be strongest for reviewing or consolidating taught material rather than introducing new content.

Training for staff and pupil tutors is essential, as are structure and time to improve the approach.

Low-attaining pupils may benefit particularly in the tutee role, but fixed roles can create dependency or low expectations if poorly designed.

The evidence concerns planned tutoring approaches, often delivered in intensive blocks, rather than a brief instruction to compare answers.

DO

Use secure, previously taught content and provide an accurate answer, model or checking guide.

Train pupils in the tutor and tutee roles, including how to prompt, explain and check.

Specify the product and make both pupils accountable for understanding.

Monitor explanations for accuracy and use an independent recheck.

Rotate or review roles where appropriate so labels do not become fixed.

DON'T

Use peers to teach unfamiliar content without sufficient knowledge or support.

Assume that placing pupils together automatically creates effective tutoring.

Use vague peer marking that focuses on presentation or praise rather than improvement.

Leave misconceptions unmonitored because the teacher has delegated the explanation.

Present the +6-month estimate as the effect of one peer-explanation prompt.

AGENT USE: Recommend structured peer tutoring for consolidation and trained peer assessment. A short peer explanation may be EEF-aligned, but the Toolkit estimate applies to the broader, structured approach.

Primary EEF sources: Toolkit: Peer tutoring; Guidance: Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning
