Evidence summary

Collaborative learning approaches

ALIASES: cooperative learning; structured group work; shared outcome; group problem-solving

BSCS COMPONENT: Drive thinking for all; Secure learning through practice; Climate and culture; Check and Adapt

EEF EVIDENCE HEADLINE: +5 months average impact; very low cost; low evidence security; 212 studies; Toolkit review updated July 2021.

EVIDENCE STATUS: Direct EEF Toolkit evidence for structured collaborative learning. Evidence security is low despite a positive average impact.

STUDENT PROBLEM: Group work is dominated by a few pupils, lower-prior-attaining pupils do not contribute, pupils divide work without sharing thinking, or discussion is socially active but instructionally weak.

MECHANISM: Well-designed shared tasks can develop explanation, demonstration, problem-solving and metacognitive skills. The benefit depends on participation, task structure, group composition and monitoring.

KEY EEF FINDINGS

Pupils need teaching, support and practice to collaborate effectively; productive collaboration does not happen automatically.

The most promising structures tend to use groups of three to five with responsibility for a joint outcome.

Paired work shows smaller average effects than groups of three to five in this evidence base, although pairs may still be useful for other purposes.

There is large unexplained variation, limited recent evidence and a risk of widening gaps if high-attaining pupils do the thinking for others.

DO

Design a genuine shared outcome that requires each pupil's thinking.

Teach and model productive discussion, explanation and disagreement.

Choose pairings or groups deliberately and review them flexibly.

Monitor who is thinking, explaining and learning - not just whether the group is busy.

Recheck individual understanding after collaborative work.

DON'T

Equate sitting together with collaborative learning.

Let one pupil complete the cognitive work while others copy or observe.

Use competition so heavily that winning displaces the learning goal.

Assume a brief Turn and Talk carries the Toolkit's +5-month estimate.

Ignore the low security and high variation in the evidence base.

AGENT USE: Use this evidence for sustained, structured group learning. For brief paired talk, oral-language and metacognition evidence may be the better attribution. Always flag the low evidence security.
